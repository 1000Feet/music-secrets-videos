# 49 — Swing Rhythm (slug: swing-rhythm)
Idea: pairs of eighth notes played long-short instead of even (roughly the first two thirds of a triplet + the last third).
Songs (part 1) — In the Mood (Glenn Miller · 1939) and Sing, Sing, Sing (Benny Goodman · 1937) are copyrighted: play only a generic swing groove, no melodies.
Why (part 2), all true:
- Straight eighths: 1-and-2-and, perfectly even. Swing: the "and" arrives late — long-short, long-short.
- The amount of swing varies; slower tempos often swing harder, very fast tempos get closer to even.
- Swing grew out of jazz in the 1920s–30s; big bands made it the dance music of the era.
- The ride cymbal pattern and the walking bass (on every beat) carry the swing.
Visual idea: two grids — straight eighths vs swung eighths, the off-beat cell drawn later; demo the same melody (an original simple phrase) straight and then swung.
Essence: delay one note by a hair, and a stiff rhythm starts to dance.

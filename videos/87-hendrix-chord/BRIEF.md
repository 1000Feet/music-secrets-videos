# 87 — The Hendrix Chord (slug: hendrix-chord)
Idea: E7#9 — a chord that is major and minor at the same time: E G# D + G (the "sharp nine", the same key as a minor third).
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted, chords only (no riffs):
- Purple Haze — The Jimi Hendrix Experience · 1967: built around E7#9. Rock guitarists nicknamed it "the Hendrix chord".
- Taxman — The Beatles · 1966: also uses a 7#9 chord.
- Jazz and R&B players used the chord before rock adopted it.
Why (part 2), all true:
- E7 contains G#, the major third. The #9 adds G natural — which sounds like the minor third. Both at once.
- Blues singers and guitarists bend between the minor and major third (blue notes). This chord freezes that bend into one grip.
- On guitar, G# sits low and G high — almost an octave apart — so instead of a muddy half-step clash, you get a gritty, biting sound.
- The flat seventh (D) adds the bluesy dominant-seventh pull.
Essence: major and minor in one chord — the blues, in a single grip.

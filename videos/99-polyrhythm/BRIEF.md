# 99 — Polyrhythm: Two Speeds at Once (slug: polyrhythm)
Idea: two different beat divisions played simultaneously: 3 against 2, or 4 against 3.
Songs (part 1) — PUBLIC DOMAIN pieces, but do NOT reconstruct melodies from memory: demonstrate with your own patterns:
- Chopin, Fantaisie-Impromptu in C-sharp minor (composed 1834, published after his death in 1855): the right hand plays four notes against the left hand's three — 4 against 3 — almost all the way through.
- Debussy, Arabesque No. 1 (1888–91): triplets in one hand against pairs of notes in the other — 3 against 2.
- West African drumming: layered patterns in 3 and in 2 are a basic principle.
Why (part 2), all true:
- 3 against 2: in the time of one beat, one hand plays 3 even notes, the other 2. They line up only on the beat; in between, every note falls in a different place (a grid of 6).
- 4 against 3: they coincide only every 12 small steps.
- A simple way to feel 3 against 2: say "nice cup of tea" — "nice" together, "cup" (3), "of" (2), "tea" (3).
- The brain hears two speeds at once, so the music feels like it's flowing in two currents: restless, alive.
- Hemiola is the cousin: 3 and 2 in a row; polyrhythm is 3 and 2 at the same time.
Essence: two speeds, one moment — music that moves in two currents at once.
Visual idea: circle:false parallel grids of 3 and 2 (and 4 and 3) with coincidences highlighted.

# 29 — Four on the Floor (slug: four-on-the-floor)
Idea: the kick drum on every beat (1, 2, 3, 4), hi-hat on the off-beats.
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted, play only a generic beat:
- Stayin' Alive — Bee Gees · 1977: about 104 beats per minute — so close to the 100–120 chest compressions per minute recommended for CPR that it's used in CPR training.
- I Feel Love — Donna Summer · 1977: four-on-the-floor plus a pulsing synth sequencer.
- One More Time — Daft Punk · 2000.
Why (part 2), all true:
- A kick on every beat is the simplest possible pulse — impossible to lose, so your body locks in (we naturally move in time with a steady beat).
- The open hi-hat on the off-beats ("ts") creates lift between the kicks, like a breath in and out.
- Around 120 BPM it matches a brisk walking pace — dance music lives there.
Essence: one, two, three, four — a beat so simple your body follows it before your mind does.

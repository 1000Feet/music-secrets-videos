# 63 — The Harmonic Minor (slug: harmonic-minor)
Idea: take A natural minor (A B C D E F G) and raise the seventh, G → G#. That's the harmonic minor: A B C D E F G# A.
Songs (part 1):
- Hava Nagila — Jewish folk song, arranged by Abraham Zvi Idelsohn (1918). It uses the "Freygish" (Phrygian dominant) scale, which is the harmonic minor started on its fifth note: E F G# A B C D. Do NOT try to play its tune; play the scale.
- Misirlou — a traditional Eastern Mediterranean song, made famous as surf rock by Dick Dale (1962) and in Pulp Fiction (1994). Same Phrygian dominant scale. Dick Dale's version is copyrighted: do not play it; play the scale.
Why (part 2), all true:
- Why raise G? In natural minor the five chord is E minor (E G B), a weak pull home. With G# it becomes E major (E G# B): G# is a half step below A, a leading tone that pulls hard into A minor.
- Classical composers (Bach, Mozart…) used that major five chord in minor keys all the time: E – Am.
- The side effect: from F to G# is three half steps between neighbouring notes — an "augmented second", a gap the major scale never has.
- That gap is what sounds "exotic" to Western ears, and it is central to many Middle Eastern, Jewish and Balkan melodies.
Essence: one raised note, added for a stronger homecoming — and a whole exotic world came with it.

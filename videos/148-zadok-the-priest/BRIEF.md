# 148 — Zadok the Priest: the Ultimate Build-Up (slug: zadok-the-priest)
Idea: a long, quiet orchestral introduction that explodes when the choir enters.
Songs (part 1) — PUBLIC DOMAIN but do NOT reconstruct Handel's music; use D major chords with your own rising broken-chord pattern:
- Handel wrote Zadok the Priest in 1727, one of four Coronation Anthems for King George II.
- It has been performed at every British coronation since, including King Charles III's in 2023.
- The UEFA Champions League anthem (Tony Britten, 1992) is an adaptation in Handel's style, based on this anthem (name only; copyrighted).
Why (part 2), all true:
- The introduction: soft, repeating broken chords in the strings over a steady bass, slowly gaining intensity — pure anticipation.
- Then, on the word "Zadok", the full choir and orchestra enter at full volume (with trumpets and timpani).
- Key: D major — bright and ideal for trumpets (as in the Hallelujah Chorus).
- Holding back the entrance makes the release overwhelming: the longer the wait, the bigger the payoff.
Essence: make them wait, then give them everything — the oldest trick in the book.
Visual idea: an intensity meter slowly climbing, then a huge flash and chord on the entrance.

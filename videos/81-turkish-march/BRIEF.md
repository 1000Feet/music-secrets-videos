# 81 — Mozart's Turkish March (slug: turkish-march)
Idea: a melody that circles around notes, climbing a chord, plus a piano pretending to be a military band.
Songs (part 1) — PUBLIC DOMAIN, you may play this melody:
- Mozart, Piano Sonata No. 11 in A major, K. 331 (published 1784), third movement "Alla turca" (Rondo alla turca).
- Opening, in A minor, in quick even notes: B4 A4 G#4 A4 C5(longer) | D5 C5 B4 C5 E5(longer) | F5 E5 D#5 E5 B5 A5 G#5 A5 B5 A5 G#5 A5 C6(long).
- It imitates the Ottoman Janissary military bands, which were fashionable in Vienna, with their bass drums, cymbals and triangles.
Why (part 2), all true:
- Each little group circles one note from a half step below and a step above (B A G# A → lands on C...). These "turns" start on A, then C, then E: together they climb the A minor chord (A C E) — an arpeggio in disguise.
- The G# and D# are raised notes pulling up into A and E — leading tones.
- In the A major section, the left hand plays fast rolled, crashing chords that imitate drums and cymbals. Some pianos of that era even had a "Janissary" pedal with a drum or bells.
- Rondo form: the main theme keeps coming back between contrasting sections.
Essence: little turns climbing a chord, a piano playing soldier — and a melody nobody forgets.

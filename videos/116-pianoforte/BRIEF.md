# 116 — Why the Piano Is Called "Piano" (slug: pianoforte)
Idea: the piano's name means "soft-loud" — its revolution was dynamics controlled by touch.
Songs (part 1) — PUBLIC DOMAIN, you may play this:
- Haydn, Symphony No. 94 "Surprise" (1791): the quiet second-movement theme is suddenly interrupted by a loud chord. Theme in C: C C E E G G E(long) | F F D D B3 B3 G3(long) — play it softly, then a sudden loud full chord.
- Bartolomeo Cristofori built the first pianos in Florence around 1700, calling it "gravicembalo col piano e forte" — a harpsichord with soft and loud.
Why (part 2), all true:
- A harpsichord plucks its strings, so pressing a key harder hardly changes the volume. A piano's hammers strike the strings: press harder, it plays louder.
- Dynamic markings: pp (pianissimo), p, mp, mf, f, ff (fortissimo); crescendo = getting louder, diminuendo = getting softer.
- The sustain pedal lifts the dampers so notes keep ringing; on a grand piano the soft pedal (una corda) shifts the hammers so they strike fewer strings.
- A modern piano has 88 keys.
Essence: the same notes, soft or loud — and the piano gave every player control over both.
Visual idea: velocity bars, dynamic markings pp → ff, the Surprise chord as a sudden spike.

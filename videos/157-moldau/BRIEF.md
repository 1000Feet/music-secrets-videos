# 157 — The Moldau: a River in Music (slug: moldau)
Idea: a symphonic poem that follows a river from its springs to the city of Prague.
Songs (part 1) — PUBLIC DOMAIN but do NOT reconstruct Smetana's melodies; use E minor chords and your own flowing patterns:
- Bedřich Smetana, Vltava (The Moldau), from the cycle Má vlast (My Homeland), composed in 1874 — the year Smetana became deaf.
- The program: two small springs (two flutes) join into a river; it passes a hunt in the forest (horns), a peasant wedding (a polka), nymphs dancing by moonlight, the St. John's Rapids, and flows broadly past Vyšehrad castle in Prague.
Why (part 2), all true:
- Two flutes start with interweaving running notes: two streams. Then other instruments join — the river grows.
- The flowing accompaniment (running notes) never stops, like the current.
- The main theme in E minor is broad and song-like; near the end it returns in E major — triumph as the river reaches Prague.
- Program music: every section paints a scene (see our Vivaldi Spring video).
Essence: two trickles become a mighty river — and the music flows from minor to major.
Visual idea: two lanes merging into many; scene cards along a timeline; E minor → E major on the circle.

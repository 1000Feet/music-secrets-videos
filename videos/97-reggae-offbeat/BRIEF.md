# 97 — Reggae: Living on the Offbeat (slug: reggae-offbeat)
Idea: in reggae, the guitar and keys play short chords only on the offbeats (the "skank"), and the drums leave beat one empty (the "one drop").
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted; play only generic grooves:
- Bob Marley & the Wailers: drummer Carlton Barrett is closely associated with the "one drop" beat.
- Jamaican music evolved from ska (early 1960s, fast, offbeat horns and guitar), to rocksteady (slower), to reggae (late 1960s).
Why (part 2), all true:
- The skank: short, choppy chords on the "and" of each beat (or on 2 and 4 in half time). Your ear fills the empty downbeats itself.
- The one drop: kick and snare (often a rimshot) land together on beat 3; beat 1 is left empty. The beat seems to "drop" on 3.
- The bass is deep and melodic, carrying the tune underneath.
- With the downbeat empty and the chords on the offbeat, the groove feels relaxed and floating, leaning back instead of pushing forward.
Essence: leave the downbeat empty, play the offbeat — and the groove starts to float.
Visual idea: circle:false 8- or 16-step grids, kick+snare on 3, chords on the offbeats.

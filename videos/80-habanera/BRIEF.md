# 80 — Carmen's Habanera (slug: habanera)
Idea: a melody that slides down by half steps over a Cuban dance rhythm.
Songs (part 1) — PUBLIC DOMAIN, you may play these elements:
- Georges Bizet, Carmen (premiered in Paris, Opéra-Comique, 1875): Carmen's aria "L'amour est un oiseau rebelle", the Habanera.
- The melody begins by sliding down a half step at a time: D5 C#5 C5 B4 Bb4 A4 (in D minor), over a repeated low D.
- The bass repeats the habanera rhythm in 2/4: dotted eighth, sixteenth, eighth, eighth (DUM–da DUM DUM).
- Bizet adapted the melody from "El Arreglito" by the Spanish composer Sebastián Yradier, believing it was a folk song; he later credited Yradier in the score.
Why (part 2), all true:
- The chromatic descent: six notes, each just a half step lower — slippery, seductive, never settling, just like Carmen.
- The habanera rhythm comes from Cuba, and is related to the tresillo (3+3+2) — the same family as many Latin and pop grooves.
- The low D never moves (a pedal point), so the sliding melody keeps rubbing against it.
- The verse is in D minor; the refrain switches to D major — dark and teasing turns bright and bold.
Essence: a melody that slips through your fingers, over a rhythm that never stops swaying.

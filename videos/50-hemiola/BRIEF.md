# 50 — The Hemiola (slug: hemiola)
Idea: regroup six pulses as 2 + 2 + 2 instead of 3 + 3 — three against two, without changing the speed.
Songs (part 1):
- "America" from West Side Story — Leonard Bernstein · 1957 · copyrighted: alternates bars felt in 6/8 (two groups of three) and 3/4 (three groups of two). Rhythm only, no melody.
- Baroque and classical composers (Handel, Brahms) love hemiolas right before a cadence (general statement; no specific melody required).
Why (part 2), all true:
- Six can be divided two ways: 3 + 3 or 2 + 2 + 2. Switching between them makes the beat seem to shift under your feet.
- The tempo of the smallest pulse never changes — only the grouping (where the accents fall) changes.
- That momentary confusion creates energy, then the music snaps back into place.
Visual idea: a 6-cell grid; accents on 1 and 4 (3 + 3) vs 1, 3, 5 (2 + 2 + 2); clap pattern with a.perc.
Essence: same six beats, regrouped — rhythm's own optical illusion.

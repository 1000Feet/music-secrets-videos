# 140 — The Science of Music Chills (slug: music-chills)
Idea: brain scans show that peak musical moments release dopamine — the same reward chemical as food and other pleasures.
Songs (part 1) — play only your own build-and-peak example (generic chords, a crescendo to a climax):
- In 2011, Valorie Salimpoor and colleagues (published in Nature Neuroscience) scanned listeners while they heard music that gave them chills.
- Musical chills (frisson) are reported by many, but not all, listeners.
Why (part 2), all true:
- The study found dopamine released in the brain's reward system at the moment of peak emotion (in an area called the nucleus accumbens).
- It also found dopamine released before the peak, during anticipation (in the caudate) — the build-up itself is rewarding.
- Common triggers of chills include unexpected harmonies, sudden changes in loudness, a new voice or instrument entering, and appoggiaturas (see our appoggiatura video).
- Music plays with expectation: predict, delay, then deliver — and the brain rewards both the waiting and the arrival.
Essence: the build-up and the drop are both rewards — your brain is paying you to listen.
Visual idea: an anticipation meter rising, a peak flash; labels ANTICIPATION (caudate) / PEAK (nucleus accumbens).

# 17 — The Major Seventh Chord (slug: major-seventh)
Idea: add the note a half step below the root to a major chord: Cmaj7 = C E G B. Sounds dreamy, floating.
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted, play only chords:
- Imagine — John Lennon · 1971 · in C: C, Cmaj7, F (intro alternates C to Cmaj7 to F).
- Don't Know Why — Norah Jones · 2002 · in Bb: Bbmaj7, Bb7, Ebmaj7, D7, Gm7, C7, F (start of the verse).
- Something — The Beatles · 1969 · in C: C, Cmaj7, C7, F.
Why (part 2), all true:
- The seventh (B) is the leading tone — it normally pulls to C — but here it just sits inside the chord next to C, unresolved. That gentle rub (a major seventh = a half step below the octave) gives the floating feeling.
- Cmaj7 contains two chords at once: C major (C E G) and E minor (E G B), stacked — bright and melancholic together.
- Jazz, bossa nova and neo-soul lean on it.
Essence: a little tension that never resolves — that's what dreamy sounds like.

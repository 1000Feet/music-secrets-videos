# 128 — Maqam: the Notes Between the Keys (slug: maqam)
Idea: Arabic music uses modes called maqamat, some with quarter tones — pitches between the piano's keys.
Songs (part 1) — play only scales and your own phrases (quarter tones can be approximated with the engine's pitch-bend tone option):
- The oud, a fretless lute, can play these in-between pitches freely.
- Improvised preludes that explore a maqam are called taqsim (general).
Why (part 2), all true:
- Maqam Rast: C D E-half-flat F G A B-half-flat C. The E and B sit halfway between flat and natural — about a quarter tone off the piano.
- Maqam Hijaz: features an augmented second (e.g. D Eb F# G) — the same exotic gap as the harmonic minor.
- A maqam is not just a scale: it has typical phrases, important notes and ways of moving between groups of notes (ajnas, building blocks of 3–5 notes).
- Twelve-note equal temperament can't play quarter tones — so a piano literally can't play Maqam Rast in tune.
Essence: music that lives between the keys — and a whole world of colours the piano can't reach.
Visual idea: circle with extra "between" dots for E-half-flat and B-half-flat; compare with C major.

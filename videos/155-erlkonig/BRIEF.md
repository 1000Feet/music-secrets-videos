# 155 — Schubert's Erlkönig: One Singer, Four Characters (slug: erlkonig)
Idea: a song where one singer plays a narrator, a father, a terrified child and a seductive spirit — over a galloping piano.
Songs (part 1) — PUBLIC DOMAIN but do NOT reconstruct Schubert's melody; use G minor chords and a relentless repeated-note triplet figure in the piano:
- Franz Schubert wrote Erlkönig in 1815, at 18, setting a poem by Goethe.
- Story: a father rides through the night with his sick son; the Erl-King, a supernatural being, tempts the boy.
Why (part 2), all true:
- The piano's right hand hammers fast repeated triplets almost throughout: the galloping horse (and a racing heartbeat). It is famously tiring to play.
- Each character has its own sound: the narrator in the middle, the father low and calm, the son high and increasingly frantic, the Erl-King sweet and gentle, often in major keys.
- Each time the boy cries "Mein Vater, mein Vater!" (My father, my father!) it is pitched higher than before — rising panic.
- At the end, the galloping stops, and the narrator says, almost spoken, that the child was dead.
Essence: one voice, four characters, a piano that never stops galloping — until it does.
Visual idea: four character cards with ranges on the keyboard; the cries stepping up on the circle; gallop grid.

# 84 — Power Chords (slug: power-chords)
Idea: rock's favourite chord has only two notes: root and fifth. No third — neither major nor minor. Written "5" (C5 = C + G).
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted, do not play the riffs; play generic power chords:
- You Really Got Me — The Kinks · 1964: a distorted power-chord riff.
- Blitzkrieg Bop — Ramones · 1976: punk built almost entirely on power chords.
- Heavy metal and grunge guitar too (general).
Why (part 2), all true:
- Distortion adds tons of overtones. With a third (ratio 5:4), those overtones clash and the chord gets muddy. The fifth (3:2) stays clean.
- The two notes of a fifth produce a "difference tone" one octave below the root (frequencies 3 and 2 → 1), so a power chord even reinforces its own bass.
- Without the third, the chord is neither happy nor sad — just strong. The singer or the riff decides the mood.
- On guitar it's one shape you can slide anywhere on the neck.
- Classical counterpoint rules forbade moving in parallel fifths... medieval organum and rock music are built on them.
Essence: drop the third, keep the fifth — pure power, no mood.

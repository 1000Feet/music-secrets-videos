# 60 — Why A = 440 Hz (slug: a440)
Idea: the standard tuning pitch: the A above middle C vibrates 440 times per second.
Facts (all true):
- A = 440 Hz was agreed at an international conference in London in 1939 and later adopted as the ISO 16 standard (1955, confirmed 1975).
- Before that, tuning varied widely from city to city and century to century; baroque-music specialists today often use A = 415 Hz, about a half step lower.
- Many orchestras, especially in Europe, tune slightly higher (around 442–443 Hz) for a brighter sound.
- Orchestras tune to the oboe's A at the start of a concert.
- The claim that 432 Hz is "natural" or healing has no scientific evidence behind it.
Visual ideas: a.big with "440 Hz", a wave/lissajous, keyboard A4 lit; compare 415 / 440 / 442.
Essence: there's nothing magic about 440 — it's an agreement, so musicians everywhere can play together.

# 142 — Funk and "The One" (slug: funk-the-one)
Idea: James Brown built funk around a heavy accent on beat one, with syncopated parts interlocking around it.
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted: play only your own funk groove (16th-note grid, choppy guitar chords, syncopated bass):
- James Brown's "Papa's Got a Brand New Bag" (1965) and "Cold Sweat" (1967) are often cited as founding funk records.
- Clyde Stubblefield's drum break on James Brown's "Funky Drummer" (1970) became one of the most widely sampled drum breaks in hip hop (say "widely sampled").
Why (part 2), all true:
- Funk lives on a 16th-note grid: every beat split in four.
- "The one": the first beat of the bar is hit hard by everyone — the anchor.
- Everything else is syncopated: short guitar chops (the "chicken scratch"), bass notes between the beats, horn stabs.
- Each instrument plays a small, repeating rhythmic part; together they lock like gears.
- Harmony is often static — one chord, often a dominant ninth — so rhythm carries the music.
Essence: hit the one, then let every part dance around it — that's funk.
Visual idea: circle:false 16-step grid with the ONE highlighted and interlocking lanes (kick, snare, guitar, bass).

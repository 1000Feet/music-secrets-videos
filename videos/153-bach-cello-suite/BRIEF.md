# 153 — Bach's Cello Suite No. 1 (slug: bach-cello-suite)
Idea: one instrument, mostly one note at a time — yet you hear full chords.
Songs (part 1) — PUBLIC DOMAIN, you may play the opening four bars (in G major, sixteenth notes):
- Bar 1: G2 D3 B3 A3 B3 D3 B3 D3 (twice). Bar 2: G2 E3 C4 B3 C4 E3 C4 E3 (twice). Bar 3: G2 F#3 C4 B3 C4 F#3 C4 F#3 (twice). Bar 4: like bar 1.
- Bach's six Cello Suites, BWV 1007–1012, were written around 1720. The Prelude of Suite No. 1 is in G major.
- The cellist Pablo Casals found an old edition of the suites in a Barcelona shop as a teenager and later made the first complete recording (1936–39), making them famous.
Why (part 2), all true:
- The low open G string returns at the start of each pattern like a bass note (a pedal point), while the upper notes outline chords.
- Implied harmony: bar 1 = G major, bar 2 = C major over G, bar 3 = D7 over G, bar 4 = G again — I, IV, V7, I.
- Your ear connects the notes into chords, even though the cello mostly plays one at a time.
- The same pattern repeated with small changes creates a hypnotic flow (compare Bach's Prelude in C).
Essence: one line of notes, and the ear builds the chords — a whole orchestra inside one cello.
Visual idea: chord shapes appearing on the circle as the broken-chord notes accumulate; G pedal tag.

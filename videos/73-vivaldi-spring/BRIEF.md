# 73 — Vivaldi's Spring (slug: vivaldi-spring)
Idea: music that paints pictures: birds, streams, thunder.
Songs (part 1) — PUBLIC DOMAIN, but do NOT reconstruct Vivaldi's melody from memory; play the harmony (E – B – E, I–V–I) and your own short birdsong trills:
- Antonio Vivaldi, The Four Seasons, published in 1725 in his Op. 8. "Spring" is Concerto No. 1 in E major.
- Each concerto came with a sonnet (possibly by Vivaldi himself) describing the scene. In Spring: birds greet the season with song, streams murmur, a storm with thunder and lightning comes — then the birds sing again.
Why (part 2), all true:
- Ritornello form: the full orchestra's opening theme keeps returning, like a refrain, between solo episodes.
- In the episodes, the solo violins paint the sonnet: trills and high repeated notes for birds, soft running notes for streams, fast tremolos and scales for thunder.
- Echo effects: phrases are repeated softly, like an echo — Baroque music often switched between loud and soft in blocks.
- A concerto plays one soloist against the whole group: the contrast is the drama.
- This kind of music, telling a story or describing a scene, is called program music.
Essence: no words, no pictures — and you still see spring.

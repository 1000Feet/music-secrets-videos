# 72 — Toccata and Fugue in D minor (slug: bach-toccata)
Idea: the most famous organ opening ever: a quick ornament, a falling run, a huge chord.
Songs (part 1) — PUBLIC DOMAIN, you may play this opening:
- Toccata and Fugue in D minor, BWV 565, traditionally attributed to J. S. Bach (some scholars question whether Bach wrote it). Opening in octaves: A5–G5–A5 (a quick ornament, a "mordent", landing on a held A), pause; then G5 F5 E5 D5, C#5 (held), D5. Pause. Then the same idea an octave lower, and lower again.
- Then a huge chord builds from the bottom: a diminished seventh chord (C# E G Bb) over a low D held in the pedals, finally resolving to D minor.
- Disney's Fantasia (1940) opens with an orchestral version (by Leopold Stokowski); it became the stereotypical "spooky organ" sound in films.
Why (part 2), all true:
- The mordent is an ornament: a quick flick to the neighbour note and back — it grabs attention instantly.
- Playing in octaves makes one line sound enormous.
- The diminished seventh chord is built only of minor thirds, perfectly symmetrical — so it has no clear home and maximum tension. It resolves into D minor.
- The pauses let the sound ring through a stone church; the organ can hold a note as long as the player wants, because it's driven by wind, not hammers.
Essence: a flick, a fall, one tense chord — and silence that rings.

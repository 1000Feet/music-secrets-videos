# 48 — The Riff (slug: the-riff)
Idea: a short repeated musical phrase (ostinato) that becomes the identity of a song.
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted, do NOT play their riffs; play a generic original riff instead:
- Seven Nation Army — The White Stripes · 2003: the "bass" riff is actually Jack White's guitar run through a pitch-shifting pedal set an octave down; today it is chanted in stadiums around the world.
- (I Can't Get No) Satisfaction — The Rolling Stones · 1965: Keith Richards played the riff through a fuzz pedal (a Gibson Maestro Fuzz-Tone).
- Smoke on the Water — Deep Purple · 1972: one of the first riffs most guitarists learn.
Why (part 2), all true:
- Repetition makes a phrase easy to remember; a riff repeated many times becomes a hook.
- Riffs are usually short (one or two bars), rhythmic and built from few notes (often a pentatonic or blues scale).
- Classical music has the same idea: an ostinato (Ravel's Boléro snare, Holst's Mars).
Essence: say it short, say it again — the riff is music's logo.

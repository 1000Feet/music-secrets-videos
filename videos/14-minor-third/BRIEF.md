# 14 — The Minor Third (slug: minor-third)
Idea: 3 half steps (A to C). Ratio 6:5. The interval of minor chords and of the playground taunt.
Songs (part 1):
- Greensleeves — Traditional (public domain), in A minor: A3 C4 D4 E4 F4 E4 D4 (first leap = minor third). Melody can be played.
- Hey Jude — The Beatles · 1968 · copyrighted: ONLY the two notes of "Hey Jude": C4 → A3 (falls a minor third), in F.
- Smoke on the Water — Deep Purple · 1972 · copyrighted: ONLY the first two notes of the riff, G → Bb (up a minor third).
- The playground taunt "nyah nyah nyah-nyah nyah" — G4 E4 A4 G4 E4 — kids everywhere sing a falling minor third (public domain).
Why (part 2), all true:
- 6:5 ratio. A minor chord = minor third on the bottom, major third on top (A C E). A major chord is the reverse (C E G): major third + minor third. Either way the two thirds add up to a perfect fifth (6:5 × 5:4 = 3:2).
- So major and minor use the same two building blocks, just swapped.
- The minor third is just three half steps; stacking minor thirds gives the diminished chord (and four of them divide the octave equally).
Essence: two thirds, swapped — that small flip is the whole difference between happy and sad.

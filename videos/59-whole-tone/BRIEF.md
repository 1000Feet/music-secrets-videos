# 59 — The Whole-Tone Scale (slug: whole-tone)
Idea: six notes, each a whole step apart: C D E F# G# A#. No half steps at all.
Examples (part 1):
- Claude Debussy, "Voiles" (Préludes, Book 1, 1909–10) · public domain: much of it is built on the whole-tone scale (you may play a whole-tone texture, not necessarily the exact notes).
- Film and TV often use whole-tone runs for dreams and flashbacks (general).
Why (part 2), all true:
- No half steps → no leading tone → nothing pulls toward a home note: the music floats.
- It divides the octave into six equal steps — on the circle, a perfect hexagon.
- Only TWO different whole-tone scales exist (C D E F# G# A# and C# D# F G A B); shifting by one more whole step gives the same notes again.
- Any chord built from it is an augmented chord (stacked major thirds).
Essence: equal steps, no gravity — the sound of a dream.

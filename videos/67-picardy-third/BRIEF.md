# 67 — The Picardy Third (slug: picardy-third)
Idea: a piece in a minor key that ends on a MAJOR chord: the third of the last chord is raised (C minor → C major: Eb becomes E).
Songs (part 1):
- Bach, Prelude in C minor, BWV 847 (The Well-Tempered Clavier, Book 1, 1722) — PUBLIC DOMAIN: in C minor, but the final chord is C major. You may play its harmonies/ending.
- Many of Bach's minor-key chorales and Baroque pieces end this way (general).
- The name "tierce de Picardie" (Picardy third) appears in Jean-Jacques Rousseau's Dictionnaire de musique (1768); where the name comes from is unclear.
Why (part 2), all true:
- Every low note contains overtones; the fifth overtone is a major third (in C: E). A minor third (Eb) on a long final chord rubs against that.
- So for centuries, a minor chord was considered not fully at rest for a final chord; composers raised the third to end on pure consonance.
- The effect: after a sad piece, one note moves up a half step at the last moment — light breaks through.
Essence: after all that darkness, one note rises half a step — and the very last chord opens like a window.

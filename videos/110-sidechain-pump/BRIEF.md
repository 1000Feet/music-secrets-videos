# 110 — The Pump: Sidechain Compression (slug: sidechain-pump)
Idea: in dance music, every time the kick hits, the other sounds duck in volume and swell back — the whole track "breathes".
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted: play only your own four-on-the-floor kick with sustained chords:
- French house: Daft Punk's One More Time (2000) is a famous example of the pumping sound.
- Call on Me — Eric Prydz · 2004: an extreme, famous pump.
Why (part 2), all true:
- A compressor turns sounds down when a signal gets loud. In "sidechain" mode, the kick drum's signal controls the compressor on other tracks (pads, bass).
- So on every kick, the chords dip; between kicks, they swell back up.
- Two effects: the kick cuts through clearly, and even long sustained chords start to pulse in rhythm.
- Show a volume envelope dipping on every beat, under a held chord.
- This video uses the same trick: the music ducks under the narrator's voice (that's how our soundtrack is mixed).
Essence: let the kick push everything else aside — and the music starts to breathe.

# 141 — The Mozart Effect: Myth or Fact? (slug: mozart-effect)
Idea: the famous claim that listening to Mozart makes you smarter — what the science actually found.
Songs (part 1) — PUBLIC DOMAIN but do NOT reconstruct Mozart's melodies; use your own bright D major texture:
- In 1993, a short paper in Nature by Frances Rauscher, Gordon Shaw and Katherine Ky reported that college students who listened to Mozart's Sonata for Two Pianos in D major, K. 448, scored slightly higher on a spatial-reasoning task.
- The media turned it into "Mozart makes babies smarter". In 1998 Georgia's governor Zell Miller proposed giving every newborn in the state a classical music CD.
Why (part 2), all true:
- The original effect was small and short-lived: it lasted only about 10–15 minutes.
- It was never about babies or general intelligence — the study tested adults on one kind of task.
- Later reviews of many studies found little or no special Mozart effect; any short boost is better explained by mood and arousal — enjoyable, energising music of any kind can do it.
- Actually learning to play music is a different question from passively listening.
Essence: Mozart won't make you a genius — but music that lifts your mood can lift your focus, for a little while.

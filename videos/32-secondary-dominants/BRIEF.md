# 32 — Secondary Dominants (slug: secondary-dominants)
Idea: a "five chord" borrowed to point at a chord other than home. In C: A7 is the five of Dm; E7 is the five of Am; D7 is the five of G.
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted except where noted, play only chords:
- Yesterday — The Beatles · 1965 · in F: F, Em7, A7, Dm — A7 is the five chord of D minor.
- Sweet Georgia Brown — 1925 · in F (public domain composition in the US since 2021): a chain D7, G7, C7, F — each chord is the five of the next.
Why (part 2), all true:
- A7 contains C#, a note outside the key of F; C# acts as a temporary leading tone and pulls up to D. For a moment Dm feels like home (that's called tonicization).
- Every major or minor chord in a key can get its own "five": that's how songs add colour without really changing key.
- Chain them and each chord falls into the next one, like the circle of fifths.
Essence: borrow a pull from outside the key, and any chord can become a home for a moment.

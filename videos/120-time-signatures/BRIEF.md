# 120 — What the Two Numbers Mean: Time Signatures (slug: time-signatures)
Idea: the top number counts beats per bar; the bottom number says which note gets the beat.
Songs (part 1) — refer to our earlier videos; play only generic patterns:
- 3/4: The Blue Danube (waltz).
- 2/4: marches like The Stars and Stripes Forever.
- 5/4 and 7/4: Take Five and Money (see our odd-meter videos) — name only.
- 4/4 is so common it's called "common time".
Why (part 2), all true:
- Top number: how many beats in each bar. Bottom number: the note value of one beat (4 = quarter note, 8 = eighth note, 2 = half note).
- 4/4: four quarter-note beats. 3/4: three. 6/8: six eighth notes, felt as two groups of three.
- 4/4 is often written as a "C". It doesn't actually come from "common": it comes from medieval notation, where a full circle meant triple time and a broken circle (a C) meant duple time.
- 2/2, "cut time", is written as a C with a vertical line through it.
- The time signature tells you where the strong beats fall — beat 1 of each bar gets the weight.
Essence: two little numbers — and they decide whether you march, waltz or rock.
Visual idea: circle:false grids for 4/4, 3/4, 2/4, 6/8 with the strong beat highlighted.

# 118 — Home, Away, Tension: Chord Functions (slug: chord-functions)
Idea: chords have jobs — tonic (home), subdominant (moving away), dominant (tension that wants home).
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted/traditional: chords only:
- La Bamba — traditional Mexican song, Ritchie Valens' 1958 hit: loops I – IV – V (C – F – G).
- Twist and Shout (popularised by the Isley Brothers, 1962, and the Beatles, 1963): also a I – IV – V loop.
- The 12-bar blues is built from I, IV and V (see our blues video).
Why (part 2), all true:
- Tonic (I): stable, the resting place.
- Subdominant / predominant (IV, also ii): moves away from home and prepares the dominant.
- Dominant (V, V7, also vii°): contains the leading tone (B in C), a half step below home, and in V7 a tritone (B–F) that resolves inward to C–E.
- Chords sharing notes can substitute: vi (A C E) shares two notes with I (C E G), so it can act like home — that's the deceptive cadence.
- Tonic → Subdominant → Dominant → Tonic is the basic "sentence" of tonal music.
Essence: three jobs — home, away, tension — and almost every song is built from them.
Visual idea: label chords T / S / D with colours; show the B→C and F→E resolutions with arcs.

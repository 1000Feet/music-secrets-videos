# 113 — Verse, Pre-Chorus, Chorus (slug: verse-chorus)
Idea: the structure behind most modern pop and rock songs.
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted: chords/generic grooves only:
- Smells Like Teen Spirit — Nirvana · 1991: quiet verses, a building pre-chorus, a loud chorus. Kurt Cobain said he was trying to rip off the Pixies' quiet-loud dynamics.
- Most chart pop since the 1960s uses some version of verse–chorus form (general).
Why (part 2), all true:
- Verse: the melody repeats but the lyrics change — it tells the story.
- Chorus: same lyrics and melody every time, usually containing the hook and often the title.
- Pre-chorus: a short section that builds tension — often on chords that point toward the chorus.
- The chorus is where the energy peaks: a higher melody, a fuller arrangement, and often a landing on the home chord.
- A bridge can add contrast before the final chorus.
- Repetition of the chorus is what makes a song memorable.
Essence: tell a little, build a little, then give the hook — and repeat until everyone sings along.

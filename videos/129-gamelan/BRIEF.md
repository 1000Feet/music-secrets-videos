# 129 — Gamelan (slug: gamelan)
Idea: Indonesian ensembles of bronze instruments with tunings that don't match the piano.
Songs (part 1) — play only your own interlocking patterns (approximate tunings with the engine's tone option if useful):
- Gamelan orchestras from Java and Bali are made mostly of bronze metallophones, gongs and drums.
- Debussy heard a Javanese gamelan at the 1889 Paris Exposition Universelle; it influenced works like his piano piece "Pagodes" (1903).
Why (part 2), all true:
- Two tuning systems: slendro (five notes, roughly equally spaced) and pelog (seven unequal notes, usually five used at a time). Neither matches Western equal temperament.
- Each gamelan is tuned as a set: tunings vary from one ensemble to another.
- Structure is cyclical: the large gong marks the end of each cycle, smaller gongs divide it.
- In Balinese gamelan, paired instruments are deliberately tuned slightly apart, creating a shimmering beat (ombak, "wave").
- Interlocking parts (kotekan): two players each play part of a fast melody that only exists when combined.
Essence: bronze, cycles and a different tuning — music that shimmers instead of resolving.
Visual idea: circle with five slendro dots spaced evenly (not on the 12 positions); cycle grid with big-gong marker.

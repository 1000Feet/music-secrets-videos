# 31 — The Augmented Chord (slug: augmented-chord)
Idea: two major thirds stacked: C+ = C E G#. Dreamy, unstable, "something's about to change".
Examples (part 1):
- Oh! Darling — The Beatles · 1969 · copyrighted: the song opens on an augmented chord (play just one generic E augmented chord, E G# B#=C, no melody).
- Used in film and TV for dream sequences and "wavy" transitions (general statement).
- Classic move (public domain idea): C → C+ → F: the G rises to G# then to A (the third of F).
Why (part 2), all true:
- Two major thirds = 8 half steps; a third major third brings you back to C: the augmented chord splits the octave into three equal parts — an equilateral TRIANGLE on the circle.
- Symmetric → no note is clearly the root. There are only FOUR different augmented chords (12 ÷ 3); rotate the triangle by one, two, three steps and the fourth step is the first again.
- The raised fifth (G#) acts like a leading tone pushing up to A — that's why C+ leads so naturally to F or Am.
Essence: a chord with no bottom and no top — pure motion waiting to land.

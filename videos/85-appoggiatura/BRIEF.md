# 85 — Why Some Songs Give You Chills: the Appoggiatura (slug: appoggiatura)
Idea: an appoggiatura (from Italian appoggiare, "to lean") is a note that doesn't belong to the chord, lands on a strong beat, and then resolves by step to a chord note.
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted, chords only:
- Someone Like You — Adele · 2011 · in A: A – C#m – F#m – D.
- In 2012 the Wall Street Journal article "Anatomy of a Tear-Jerker" asked why this song makes people cry, and pointed to its appoggiaturas.
Why (part 2), all true:
- Demonstrate with your OWN examples: over a C chord, sing D on the beat (clash), then fall to C (release). Over G, sing C, then fall to B.
- It's tension and release in miniature: the brain expects a chord note, gets a "wrong" one, then gets the resolution slightly late.
- Psychologist John Sloboda (1991) asked listeners which passages gave them tears or shivers; many of those passages contained appoggiaturas. Others had unexpected harmonies or sudden changes in loudness.
- A chain of appoggiaturas in a row creates wave after wave of tension and release.
Essence: lean on the wrong note, then let it fall — that tiny release is what gives you chills.

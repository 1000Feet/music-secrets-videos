# 74 — Chopin's Nocturne in E-flat (slug: chopin-nocturne)
Idea: a piano that sings like an opera voice, with time that stretches and breathes: rubato.
Songs (part 1) — PUBLIC DOMAIN, but do NOT reconstruct Chopin's melody from memory; play the accompaniment pattern (12/8: low bass note, then two rocking chords) on Eb – Bb7 – Eb and your own short original singing line:
- Frédéric Chopin, Nocturne in E-flat major, Op. 9 No. 2, published in 1832, in 12/8 time.
- The genre "nocturne" (night piece) for piano was created by the Irish composer John Field; Chopin made it famous.
Why (part 2), all true:
- The left hand rocks gently: a deep bass note, then chords above, in a slow 12/8 lilt.
- The right hand sings. Chopin loved Italian opera (bel canto) and wrote piano melodies like a voice.
- Each time the melody returns, it comes back more decorated: trills, turns, fast little runs — the way an opera singer ornaments a repeated phrase.
- Rubato ("stolen time"): the melody can lean back or push ahead while the accompaniment keeps a steadier pulse, so the music breathes.
Essence: a steady rocking below, a voice that breathes above — time itself becomes expressive.

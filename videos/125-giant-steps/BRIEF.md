# 125 — Giant Steps (slug: giant-steps)
Idea: John Coltrane's tune jumps between three keys a major third apart, dividing the octave into three equal parts.
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted: chords only, no melody:
- Giant Steps — John Coltrane · recorded 1959, released 1960. Opening chords: Bmaj7 D7 | Gmaj7 Bb7 | Ebmaj7 | Am7 D7 | Gmaj7 Bb7 | Ebmaj7 F#7 | Bmaj7 | Fm7 Bb7 | Ebmaj7 ...
- Coltrane also used this cycle (the "Coltrane changes") to reharmonise standard tunes.
Why (part 2), all true:
- The three key centres — B, G and E-flat — are each a major third apart. Together they form an augmented triad: on the circle, a perfect equilateral triangle.
- Each new key is reached through its own dominant seventh chord: D7 → G, Bb7 → Eb, F#7 → B.
- Because the keys are evenly spaced, no key feels like "home" for long: the tune keeps spinning.
- It's played at a very fast tempo, making it a famous test for improvisers.
Essence: three keys, equally spaced — and harmony turns into a spinning wheel.
Visual idea: equilateral triangle B–G–Eb on the circle; walkers jumping between corners.

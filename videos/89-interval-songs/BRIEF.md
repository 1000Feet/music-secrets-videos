# 89 — Learn Every Interval with Famous Songs (slug: interval-songs)
Idea: an ear-training cheat sheet: the first two notes of famous tunes.
Songs (part 1) — play only the first two (or three) notes of each:
- Minor 2nd (1 half step): Jaws theme — John Williams · 1975 (copyrighted: two alternating notes only, E–F).
- Major 2nd (2): Happy Birthday — "Hap-py BIRTH-" (G G A).
- Minor 3rd (3): Greensleeves — "A-las" (A–C) (public domain).
- Major 3rd (4): When the Saints Go Marching In — "Oh when" (C–E) (public domain).
- Perfect 4th (5): Here Comes the Bride — Wagner's Bridal Chorus, 1850 (D–G) (public domain).
- Tritone (6): Maria — West Side Story, 1957 (copyrighted: two notes only).
- Perfect 5th (7): Twinkle Twinkle Little Star (C–G) (public domain).
- Major 6th (9): My Bonnie Lies over the Ocean — "My Bon-" (G–E) (public domain).
- Octave (12): Somewhere Over the Rainbow — 1939 (copyrighted: two notes only, "Some-where", C–C').
Why (part 2), all true:
- Count half steps on the circle: each interval is a fixed distance, whatever note you start on. Show each as an arc on the chromatic circle.
- Small intervals (seconds) move melodies; big ones (sixths, octaves) make dramatic openings — that's why so many anthems open with a leap.
- Your memory of songs is relative: you remember the distances, not the exact notes, so you can sing Happy Birthday starting anywhere.
- Musicians use exactly this trick for ear training.
Essence: every melody is a chain of distances — learn a few songs, and you can hear them all.

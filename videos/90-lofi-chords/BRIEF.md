# 90 — Why Lo-fi Sounds So Relaxing (slug: lofi-chords)
Idea: the lo-fi hip hop sound: jazzy seventh and ninth chords, a lazy swung beat, and softened sound.
Songs (part 1) — general, no copyrighted material to play; use your own chords and beats:
- The "lofi hip hop radio – beats to relax/study to" 24/7 YouTube stream (Lofi Girl, formerly ChilledCow, since 2017) made the genre a worldwide study soundtrack.
- Key influences: the producers J Dilla, known for loose, off-grid drum programming, and Nujabes, known for jazz-based hip hop.
Why (part 2), all true:
- Chords: major sevenths, minor sevenths and ninths (e.g. Fmaj7 – Em7 – Dm9 – Cmaj9) borrowed from jazz. Major and minor seventh chords contain no tritone, so nothing demands to resolve — the music just floats.
- Progressions often hover, like ii–V moves that never land on the home chord.
- Slow tempos (roughly 70–90 BPM) and a lazy swing: the drums sit slightly off the grid, like a human playing behind the beat.
- The sound is filtered: highs rolled off (low-pass), vinyl crackle and tape wobble — warm, muffled, like a memory.
Essence: chords that never ask to resolve, a beat that leans back — music designed to let you breathe.

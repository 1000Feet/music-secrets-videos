# 27 — 7/4 Time (slug: seven-four-time)
Idea: seven beats per bar, usually felt as 4 + 3 or 2 + 2 + 3.
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted, play only a generic 7/4 groove:
- Money — Pink Floyd · 1973: the bass riff is in 7/4 (the guitar solo famously switches to 4/4).
- Solsbury Hill — Peter Gabriel · 1977: mostly in 7/4.
- Unsquare Dance — Dave Brubeck · 1961: 7/4 with hand claps.
Why (part 2), all true:
- Seven splits into uneven groups (4 + 3, or 2 + 2 + 3): every bar ends one beat "early", like a skipped step.
- Repeat it and the skip becomes a groove your body learns — that's why it feels catchy, not chaotic.
- Visual idea: 7-cell grid with accent groups; compare with a 4/4 bar.
Essence: lose one beat from eight, and a simple loop starts to stumble beautifully.

# 104 — The Bass Your Phone Can't Play (slug: missing-fundamental)
Idea: tiny speakers can't reproduce deep bass, yet you still hear the bass line: your brain fills in the missing note.
Songs (part 1):
- Any bass-heavy song played on a phone speaker (general — no copyrighted music; use your own bass line).
- Pipe organs: builders fake very low notes by sounding two higher pipes together, a note and its fifth above (a "resultant" stop).
Why (part 2), all true:
- A note at 100 Hz has overtones at 200, 300, 400, 500 Hz...
- Remove the 100 Hz completely and play only 200, 300, 400 Hz: the brain still hears a pitch of 100 Hz — the spacing between the overtones. This is the "missing fundamental".
- That's why a bass line is still recognisable on a phone or laptop speaker that can't physically produce those low frequencies.
- Producers add harmonics to bass sounds (saturation, distortion) so the bass can be "heard" on small speakers.
- The organ trick works the same way: two pipes a fifth apart (like 2× and 3×) imply a fundamental an octave below the lower one.
Essence: the bass you hear on your phone isn't there — your brain is playing it.
Visual idea: frequency bars/harmonics; the fundamental bar fades out while the perceived pitch label stays.

# 114 — Bohemian Rhapsody: a Hit with No Chorus (slug: bohemian-rhapsody)
Idea: a six-minute single built from contrasting sections instead of a repeating chorus.
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted: do NOT play any melody, riff or exact chords; use generic textures to illustrate each section type:
- Bohemian Rhapsody — Queen · 1975, written by Freddie Mercury. About six minutes long.
- It was No. 1 in the UK for nine weeks (1975–76) and No. 1 again in 1991 after Mercury's death.
- Sections: a cappella intro, piano ballad, guitar solo, mock-operatic section, hard rock section, quiet outro.
Why (part 2), all true:
- No section returns as a chorus. Music that keeps moving forward without repeating sections is called "through-composed" — like opera.
- Each section has its own texture, tempo and mood: voices alone, piano and voice, full band, layered choir-like vocals, heavy rock.
- It moves through several keys, so each section feels like a new scene.
- The contrast itself becomes the hook: you wait to hear what comes next.
Essence: no chorus, all story — six scenes in six minutes.

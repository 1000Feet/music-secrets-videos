# 134 — The TR-808: the Drum Machine That Changed Music (slug: tr-808)
Idea: a drum machine that flopped at first, then became the sound of hip hop.
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted: play only your own 808-style patterns (deep sine kick with long decay, handclap, hats, cowbell via engine tone/perc):
- Roland TR-808 Rhythm Composer, introduced in 1980.
- Early hits: Marvin Gaye's "Sexual Healing" (1982) and Afrika Bambaataa & the Soulsonic Force's "Planet Rock" (1982).
- Its booming kick later became the bass of trap music (see our trap video).
Why (part 2), all true:
- It generated sounds with analog circuits instead of playing recordings (samples), so its drums sound synthetic rather than real.
- The kick drum is essentially a decaying sine wave; its decay can be made very long, so it doubles as a deep bass note.
- At first musicians found it unrealistic and it sold poorly; once it was cheap on the second-hand market, hip hop and electro producers embraced it.
- You programmed it on a step sequencer: 16 buttons, one per sixteenth note, lit for each hit.
Essence: a "failed" drum machine with a fake-sounding kick — and it shaped decades of music.
Visual idea: 16-step grid with kick, snare/clap, hat, cowbell lanes; kick shown as a long decaying bar.

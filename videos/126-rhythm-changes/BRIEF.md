# 126 — Rhythm Changes (slug: rhythm-changes)
Idea: the chords of one Gershwin song became one of the most common frameworks in jazz.
Songs (part 1) — chords only, no melodies:
- I Got Rhythm — George & Ira Gershwin · 1930 (from the musical Girl Crazy). AABA, 32 bars, usually in B-flat.
- Bebop musicians wrote new melodies on its chords: Charlie Parker's "Anthropology", Sonny Rollins' "Oleo", Duke Ellington's "Cotton Tail".
Why (part 2), all true:
- The A sections loop a fast turnaround: I – vi – ii – V (Bb – Gm7 – Cm7 – F7), round and round.
- The bridge is a chain of dominant seventh chords around the circle of fifths: D7 – G7 – C7 – F7, each one the five of the next (secondary dominants), landing back on Bb.
- A simple, strong harmonic skeleton that musicians knew by heart — so new melodies and solos could be invented on top of it.
- Writing a new tune over existing chords was common in jazz (a "contrafact").
Essence: one song's skeleton, a thousand new melodies — that's rhythm changes.
Visual idea: AABA blocks; on the circle, the bridge's roots walking D → G → C → F.

# 52 — One Tune, Three Songs (slug: twinkle-abc)
Public domain — can be played freely.
Facts: Twinkle Twinkle Little Star, the Alphabet Song (ABC) and Baa Baa Black Sheep share the same melody, from the 18th-century French song "Ah! vous dirai-je, maman". Mozart wrote 12 variations on it (K. 265, early 1780s).
Melody in C: C4 C4 G4 G4 A4 A4 G4 | F4 F4 E4 E4 D4 D4 C4 | G4 G4 F4 F4 E4 E4 D4 (x2) | C4 C4 G4 G4 A4 A4 G4 | F4 F4 E4 E4 D4 D4 C4.
Facts usable in part 2:
- Form A B B A: the opening phrase returns at the end — perfect for memory.
- The first leap is a perfect fifth (C to G), then the melody steps down the scale home: tension up, gravity down.
- Each line ends on a long note, leaving room to breathe — and to fit different words.
- Mozart's variations show how one simple tune can be decorated endlessly (you can play a decorated version with running notes).
Essence: a leap up, a staircase down, and a return — so simple that three songs and Mozart all used it.

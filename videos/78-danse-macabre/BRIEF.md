# 78 — Danse Macabre (slug: danse-macabre)
Idea: Death plays the violin at midnight — tuned to the devil's interval.
Songs (part 1) — PUBLIC DOMAIN, you may play these elements:
- Camille Saint-Saëns, Danse macabre (1874), a symphonic poem based on a poem by Henri Cazalis: at midnight Death appears, plays his fiddle, and skeletons dance until dawn.
- The harp plays a single note (D) twelve times: the clock striking midnight.
- The solo violin plays the open strings A and E flat together, again and again: Death tuning his fiddle.
- The waltz is in G minor, 3/4.
Why (part 2), all true:
- Saint-Saëns asked the soloist to tune the violin's top string down from E to E flat ("scordatura"). So the open strings A and E flat form a tritone — six half steps, the "devil's interval" (see the circle: exactly opposite).
- The xylophone imitates rattling bones.
- He quotes the "Dies irae", the medieval chant for the dead (public domain; opening notes F E F D E C D D) — a musical symbol of death.
- At dawn the oboe plays a rooster's crow, and the skeletons flee.
Essence: one detuned string turns a violin into Death's instrument — the tritone does the rest.

# 57 — Blue Notes (slug: blue-notes)
Idea: the flattened third, fifth and seventh (in C: Eb, Gb, Bb) bent into a major context. The blues scale in C: C Eb F Gb G Bb.
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted, do not play riffs:
- Smoke on the Water — Deep Purple · 1972: the second phrase of the famous riff adds the flat fifth (Db in the key of G) — the blue note.
- The flatted fifth is everywhere in blues and rock guitar solos (general).
Why (part 2), all true:
- Singers and guitarists often bend between the minor and major third — blue notes live between the piano keys.
- The flat five sits right in the middle of the octave — the tritone — which gives it its gritty edge.
- Over a major chord a minor third clashes deliberately: the friction is the "blues" feeling.
- Blue notes come from African-American musical traditions that shaped the blues.
Essence: notes "between the keys" — the sound of the blues is a deliberate clash.

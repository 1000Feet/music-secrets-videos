# 136 — Guitar Harmonics: Touch, Don't Press (slug: guitar-harmonics)
Idea: lightly touching a string at simple fractions of its length produces bell-like overtones.
Songs (part 1) — play only demonstrations (use engine tone for bell-like pure harmonics):
- Guitarists play natural harmonics by touching (not pressing) the string over certain frets and plucking.
Why (part 2), all true:
- Touch at the 12th fret (half the string): the string vibrates in two halves → the 2nd harmonic, one octave above the open string.
- Touch at the 7th fret (one third): three segments → the 3rd harmonic, an octave and a fifth above.
- Touch at the 5th fret (one quarter): four segments → the 4th harmonic, two octaves above.
- Near the 4th fret (one fifth): five segments → the 5th harmonic, two octaves and a major third above.
- The light touch creates a still point (a node) and kills the vibrations that would need to move there.
- Guitarists tune with harmonics: the 5th-fret harmonic of one string should match the 7th-fret harmonic of the next — if not, you hear beats (see our beats video).
Essence: a light touch at the right spot — and the string reveals the harmonic series hidden inside it.
Visual idea: a string drawn as a.grid segments dividing into 2, 3, 4, 5 parts; harmonics shown on the circle (C, C, G, C, E).

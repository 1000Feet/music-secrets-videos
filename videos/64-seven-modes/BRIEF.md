# 64 — Seven Modes, One Scale (slug: seven-modes)
Idea: take the seven white keys and start on each one: C Ionian, D Dorian, E Phrygian, F Lydian, G Mixolydian, A Aeolian, B Locrian. Same notes, seven moods.
Songs (part 1):
- Ionian = the major scale: most pop songs.
- Dorian: Scarborough Fair (traditional English ballad, PUBLIC DOMAIN; the traditional tune is usually sung in Dorian). Do not play Simon & Garfunkel's arrangement; you may play scale fragments only.
- Mixolydian: Norwegian Wood — The Beatles · 1965 (copyrighted, name only).
- Aeolian = the natural minor scale.
- Phrygian: the dark sound of flamenco and many metal riffs (general).
Why (part 2), all true:
- The notes don't change — only home does. So the two half steps land in different places relative to home.
- Each mode has a signature note compared with major or minor: Dorian's raised sixth, Phrygian's flat second, Lydian's sharp fourth, Mixolydian's flat seventh.
- From brightest to darkest: Lydian, Ionian, Mixolydian, Dorian, Aeolian, Phrygian, Locrian — each step lowers exactly one note.
- The names come from ancient Greek regions, but the medieval church modes don't match how the Greeks used those names.
Essence: seven notes, seven moods — the home you choose changes everything.

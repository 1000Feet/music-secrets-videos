# 16 — Major vs Minor (slug: major-vs-minor)
Idea: change one note by a half step and happy turns sad. C major = C E G, C minor = C Eb G.
Part 1 demo (public domain): Twinkle Twinkle Little Star in C major: C4 C4 G4 G4 A4 A4 G4 | F4 F4 E4 E4 D4 D4 C4;
in C minor: C4 C4 G4 G4 Ab4 Ab4 G4 | F4 F4 Eb4 Eb4 D4 D4 C4 (same tune, sad).
Minor-key hits (state only the key): Billie Jean — Michael Jackson · 1983 · F# minor; Losing My Religion — R.E.M. · 1991 · A minor;
Eleanor Rigby — The Beatles · 1966 · E minor. Play only their chord shapes generically (e.g. a minor vamp), no melodies.
Why (part 2), all true and carefully worded:
- The only difference is the third: E (4 half steps above C) or Eb (3 half steps).
- The major chord is built into the harmonic series (4:5:6); the minor chord is not as simple (10:12:15).
- Some research finds that sad speech moves in smaller pitch steps, close to a minor third — part of why minor can sound sad. But culture plays a big role too: it is not a universal law.
- Mirror fact: a minor chord is a major chord flipped (same intervals, upside down).
Essence: one half step, and the whole mood flips — the smallest change with the biggest emotional effect.

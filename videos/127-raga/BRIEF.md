# 127 — The Indian Raga (slug: raga)
Idea: a raga is more than a scale: it's a melodic personality with rules, moods and even a time of day.
Songs (part 1) — play only scales/your own phrases over a drone:
- Ravi Shankar brought the sitar and Indian classical music to Western audiences; George Harrison played sitar on the Beatles' "Norwegian Wood" (1965) and later studied with Shankar.
- Raga Yaman: a classic evening raga, using a raised fourth (like the Lydian mode): C D E F# G A B (in C).
Why (part 2), all true:
- A raga defines a set of notes, but also how to move: the ascending form (aroha) and descending form (avaroha) can differ.
- It has characteristic phrases and important notes that make it recognisable.
- Each raga is associated with a mood (rasa), and many with a time of day or a season.
- The tanpura drone holds the home note and the fifth underneath (see our drone video).
- Rhythm comes from tala, a repeating cycle: teentaal has 16 beats (4+4+4+4), and musicians meet on beat one, the sam.
- North Indian (Hindustani) and South Indian (Carnatic) traditions both use ragas.
Essence: not just which notes, but how they behave — a raga is a scale with a soul.

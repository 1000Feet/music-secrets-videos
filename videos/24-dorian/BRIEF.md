# 24 — The Dorian Mode (slug: dorian)
Idea: a minor scale with a raised sixth. D Dorian = the white keys from D to D (D E F G A B C). Natural minor would have Bb.
Songs (part 1):
- Scarborough Fair — Traditional · public domain · D Dorian: opening D4 D4 A4 A4 A4 E4 F4 E4 D4 (melody can be played).
- Oye Como Va — Tito Puente · 1962 (Santana · 1970) · copyrighted: the two-chord vamp Am7 → D9 (A Dorian). Chords only.
- So What — Miles Davis · 1959: a whole tune on D Dorian (then up a half step to Eb Dorian). Chords/scale only.
- Get Lucky — Daft Punk · 2013 · B Dorian: Bm7, D, F#m7, E. Chords only.
Why (part 2), all true:
- The only difference from natural minor is the sixth note, raised a half step (B instead of Bb in D).
- That raised sixth turns the IV chord major in a minor key: in A Dorian, D minor becomes D major (with F#); in B Dorian the fourth chord is E major (with G#). That minor-to-major-four vamp is the Dorian fingerprint.
- Result: still minor, but brighter, cooler, hopeful — loved by folk, jazz and funk.
Essence: minor with the lights left on — one raised note changes the whole atmosphere.

# 53 — The Hallelujah Chorus, decoded (slug: hallelujah-chorus)
Public domain (George Frideric Handel, Messiah, premiered in Dublin in 1742). Do not try to reproduce the exact melody; play D major block chords in the rhythm of the repeated word (strong short chords) and an imitative passage idea with an original motif.
Facts usable:
- Key: D major — bright for trumpets and timpani, which join for the big moments.
- Handel alternates two textures: everyone singing the same rhythm in big chords (homophony) and voices entering one after another with overlapping lines (polyphony). The contrast is what makes it thrilling.
- Tradition: audiences stand during the chorus; a famous story says King George II stood up at a London performance, but there is no reliable evidence for it — say it is a legend.
- Handel composed the whole Messiah in about three and a half weeks (1741).
Essence: alternate one voice with many voices, add trumpets — and a chorus becomes a celebration.

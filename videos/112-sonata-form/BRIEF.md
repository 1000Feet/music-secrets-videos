# 112 — Sonata Form: Music as a Story (slug: sonata-form)
Idea: the classic plan for first movements of symphonies and sonatas: exposition, development, recapitulation.
Songs (part 1) — PUBLIC DOMAIN but do NOT reconstruct Mozart's themes; use chords and your own short themes:
- Mozart, Symphony No. 40 in G minor, K. 550 (1788): its first movement is in sonata form. In the exposition the second theme is in B-flat major (the relative major); in the recapitulation it returns in G minor.
- Haydn, Mozart and Beethoven used sonata form for most first movements of symphonies, sonatas and string quartets (general).
Why (part 2), all true:
- Exposition: a first theme in the home key, then a contrasting second theme in a different key (often the dominant, or the relative major in minor-key pieces).
- Development: the themes are broken into fragments and pushed through many keys — the conflict.
- Recapitulation: both themes come back, now both in the home key — the conflict is resolved.
- Often a coda (tail) closes the movement.
- It works like a story: home, journey, struggle, return.
Essence: two themes, two keys, one conflict — and the return home makes it all make sense.
Visual idea: a timeline with E / D / R blocks; on the circle, show keys moving away and back.

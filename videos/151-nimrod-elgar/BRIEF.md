# 151 — Nimrod and the Enigma Variations (slug: nimrod-elgar)
Idea: a set of variations where each one is a musical portrait of a friend — and a riddle never solved.
Songs (part 1) — PUBLIC DOMAIN but do NOT reconstruct Elgar's melodies; use E-flat major chords and your own slow theme:
- Edward Elgar, Enigma Variations (1899): a theme and 14 variations, each portraying someone close to him — friends, his wife, and finally himself.
- Variation IX, "Nimrod", portrays his friend and publisher Augustus Jaeger. "Jäger" means hunter in German; Nimrod is the mighty hunter of the Bible.
- Nimrod is often played at remembrance ceremonies in Britain, such as at the Cenotaph.
Why (part 2), all true:
- The "enigma": Elgar hinted that another, well-known theme "goes" with his main theme without being played — and never revealed which. People still debate it.
- Nimrod is slow, in E-flat major, and grows from very quiet to a powerful climax, then fades away.
- Variations as portraits: rhythm, tempo and character change to suggest each person's personality (see our theme-and-variations video).
Essence: fourteen friends, one theme, and a riddle — music as a photo album.
Visual idea: 14 small portrait cards; a slow dynamic arc for Nimrod.

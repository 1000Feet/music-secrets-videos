# 159 — One Melody, Many Anthems (slug: god-save-the-king)
Idea: the tune of "God Save the King" also serves as other countries' songs.
Songs (part 1) — PUBLIC DOMAIN, you may play the first phrase (in G, 3/4): G G A | F#(dotted) G A | B B C | B(dotted) A G | A G F# | G(long).
- The melody's origin is unknown; it was first published in 1744.
- In the US, "My Country, 'Tis of Thee" (lyrics by Samuel Francis Smith, 1831) uses the same melody.
- Liechtenstein's national anthem, "Oben am jungen Rhein", also uses it.
Why (part 2), all true:
- 3/4 time, slow and stately: a dignified dance rhythm.
- An unusual phrase structure: the first phrase is 6 bars, the second 8 — not the usual 4 + 4.
- The melody moves mostly by step within a small range — easy for a crowd to sing.
- Simple melodies without words-specific rhythms are easy to give new lyrics: that's why one tune can serve several countries.
Essence: one simple, singable tune — and several nations sing it as their own.
Visual idea: the 6 + 8 bar structure as blocks; walker on the circle following the stepwise melody.

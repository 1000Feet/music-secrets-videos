# 115 — Why Tempo Markings Are in Italian (slug: tempo-italian)
Idea: Largo, Adagio, Andante, Allegro, Presto — and the metronome.
Songs (part 1) — PUBLIC DOMAIN, but do NOT reconstruct melodies; use a simple original pattern at different tempos:
- Beethoven's Moonlight Sonata, first movement: marked Adagio sostenuto.
- Beethoven's Fifth Symphony, first movement: marked Allegro con brio ("fast, with vigour").
Why (part 2), all true:
- Largo: very slow, broad. Adagio: slow ("at ease"). Andante: walking pace. Moderato: moderate. Allegro: fast (literally "cheerful"). Presto: very fast.
- Italian music dominated Europe in the 17th and 18th centuries — opera, violin making, music printing — so Italian terms became the international language of music.
- Johann Maelzel patented the mechanical metronome in 1815.
- Beethoven was one of the first major composers to add metronome markings to his works; in 1817 he published markings for his first eight symphonies.
- Metronome markings use BPM: beats per minute (60 BPM = one beat per second).
Essence: one word on the page sets the heartbeat of the whole piece.
Visual idea: a tempo ladder from Largo to Presto with a ticking metronome/grid; same pattern played faster and faster.

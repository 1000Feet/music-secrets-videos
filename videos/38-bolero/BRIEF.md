# 38 — Boléro, decoded (slug: bolero)
Facts: Maurice Ravel, Boléro, 1928. Now public domain in many countries — but to be safe, do NOT play its melody; you may play the snare rhythm idea and C major harmony.
Snare ostinato (3/4, two bars): beat 1: eighth + triplet sixteenths figure… use a simplified version: in each bar hit on 1, the "and" of 1 with a triplet, 2, the "and" of 2 with a triplet, 3, 3-and — keep it recognisably bolero-like, all with a.perc('snare').
Facts usable:
- One continuous crescendo, about 15 minutes long, from very quiet to full orchestra.
- The snare drum repeats the same two-bar rhythm the whole time (often counted as 169 times).
- The melody also repeats, passed from instrument to instrument; only the colour (orchestration) and the volume change.
- It stays in C major until, near the very end, it suddenly lurches into E major — then crashes back to C and collapses.
- Ravel himself called it "a piece for orchestra without music" — an experiment in pure orchestration.
Essence: no development, no new themes — just repetition and growing intensity, and it hypnotizes the whole world.
Visual idea: a grid of repeated bars filling up while a "volume" bar or circle glow grows; a sudden rotation of the scale from C to E at the end.

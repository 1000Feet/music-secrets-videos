# 149 — The Two Wedding Marches (slug: wedding-marches)
Idea: "Here Comes the Bride" and Mendelssohn's Wedding March — where they come from and why they work.
Songs (part 1) — PUBLIC DOMAIN; you may play the Bridal Chorus' first four notes only (a rising fourth then repeats: in B-flat, F Bb Bb Bb); otherwise chords only:
- Wagner's Bridal Chorus ("Here Comes the Bride"), from the opera Lohengrin (1850). In the opera, the marriage quickly goes wrong.
- Mendelssohn's Wedding March, from his music for A Midsummer Night's Dream (1842).
- The two became a wedding tradition after both were played at the 1858 wedding of Queen Victoria's daughter Victoria to Prince Frederick William of Prussia.
Why (part 2), all true:
- The Bridal Chorus: slow, steady 4/4, opening with a rising perfect fourth — calm and solemn, for walking in.
- Mendelssohn's march: opens with a bright trumpet fanfare of triplets, in C major — fast and festive, for walking out.
- A rising fourth sounds like a call or announcement (see our interval cheat sheet: "Here Comes the Bride" is the classic perfect fourth).
- Slow and solemn going in, bright and fast coming out: the music tells the story of the ceremony.
Essence: a calm fourth to walk in, a trumpet fanfare to walk out.
Visual idea: two cards IN / OUT; perfect fourth arc on the circle; fanfare triplet grid.

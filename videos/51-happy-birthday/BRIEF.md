# 51 — Happy Birthday, decoded (slug: happy-birthday)
Public domain — can be played freely.
Facts: the melody comes from "Good Morning to All" by the sisters Mildred and Patty Hill (1893). For decades it was claimed to be under copyright; in 2015 a US court ruled the claim invalid and the case was settled in 2016 — it is now public domain.
Melody in C (3/4, starts on a pickup): G4 G4 A4 G4 C5 B4 | G4 G4 A4 G4 D5 C5 | G4 G4 G5 E5 C5 B4 A4 | F5 F5 E5 C5 D5 C5.
Facts usable in part 2:
- It's a waltz (3/4) that starts on a pickup: "Hap-py" leads into the strong beat.
- Each phrase reaches one step higher (C, then D, then the high G) — a staircase of climaxes.
- The third phrase leaps a full octave (G4 to G5 on "Happy birthday dear…") — the reason most people can't sing it in tune: its range is more than an octave.
- The harmony is just I, IV and V (C, F, G).
Essence: the world's most-sung song is built from a pickup, three chords and one daring leap.

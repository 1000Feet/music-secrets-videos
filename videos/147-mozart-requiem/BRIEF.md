# 147 — Mozart's Unfinished Requiem (slug: mozart-requiem)
Idea: the mass for the dead that Mozart was writing when he died — and the Lacrimosa he barely started.
Songs (part 1) — PUBLIC DOMAIN but do NOT reconstruct Mozart's melody; use D minor chords in 12/8 and your own rising chromatic line:
- Requiem in D minor, K. 626. Mozart died in December 1791, leaving it unfinished.
- It had been commissioned anonymously — by Count Franz von Walsegg, who wanted to present it as his own work.
- Mozart's pupil Franz Xaver Süssmayr completed it.
- In the Lacrimosa, Mozart wrote only the first eight bars before his death.
- The film Amadeus (1984) popularised a legend that Salieri poisoned Mozart — there's no evidence for it.
Why (part 2), all true:
- D minor: a dark key Mozart used for some of his most dramatic music (general).
- The Lacrimosa moves in 12/8: four slow beats, each divided in three — a rocking, sighing pulse.
- A rising chromatic line (half step after half step) builds tension on the words about rising from the ashes (qua resurget).
- The text "Lacrimosa dies illa" means "That day of tears".
Essence: eight bars, a rising line of half steps — and the music stops where Mozart's life did.
Visual idea: circle with D minor; a walker climbing chromatically; a "BAR 8" marker where Mozart's writing ends.

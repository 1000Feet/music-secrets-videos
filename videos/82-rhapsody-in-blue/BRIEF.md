# 82 — Rhapsody in Blue (slug: rhapsody-in-blue)
Idea: jazz walks into the concert hall — announced by a clarinet glissando.
Songs (part 1) — the composition is PUBLIC DOMAIN in the US since 2020, but do NOT reconstruct the themes from memory. You may play: a low trill on F3, then a fast rising chromatic run up to Bb5 (standing in for the glissando), and blues-inflected chords (Bb7, Eb7) of your own:
- George Gershwin, Rhapsody in Blue, premiered on 12 February 1924 at Aeolian Hall, New York, by Paul Whiteman's orchestra, with Gershwin himself at the piano. Orchestrated by Ferde Grofé.
- It opens with a clarinet trill and a long, smooth upward slide (glissando). The slide was the clarinettist Ross Gorman's idea, first played as a joke in rehearsal; Gershwin kept it.
Why (part 2), all true:
- A glissando slides through every pitch in between, like a voice or a trombone — something a piano can't do. It sounds playful, cheeky, jazzy.
- Gershwin filled a classical piece with blue notes: the flat third and flat seventh (see the blues scale).
- It mixes jazz rhythm and harmony with the form of a concert piece for piano and orchestra.
- A rhapsody is a free, episodic form: themes come and go, like improvisation.
Essence: one cheeky slide — and jazz and classical music meet in the same room.

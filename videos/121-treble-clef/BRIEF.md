# 121 — How to Read Notes in 90 Seconds (slug: treble-clef)
Idea: the staff, the treble and bass clefs, and the classic memory tricks.
Songs (part 1) — PUBLIC DOMAIN, you may play: Twinkle Twinkle in C (C C G G A A G) shown as positions on the staff.
- The five-line staff descends from the lines introduced by Guido d'Arezzo in the 11th century (see our major-scale video).
Why (part 2), all true:
- The treble clef (G clef) is a stylised letter G; its spiral curls around the second line, which is G.
- The bass clef (F clef) is a stylised letter F; its two dots surround the fourth line, which is F.
- Treble clef lines, bottom to top: E G B D F ("Every Good Boy Does Fine"); spaces: F A C E (spells "FACE").
- Bass clef lines: G B D F A ("Good Boys Do Fine Always"); spaces: A C E G ("All Cows Eat Grass").
- Middle C sits on a short extra line (a ledger line) between the two staves of the grand staff, as used for piano.
- Each line and each space is one step of the scale: line, space, line, space...
Essence: five lines, two clefs, a few silly sentences — and you can read music.

# 86 — The "Just the Two of Us" Progression (slug: just-the-two-of-us)
Idea: a four-chord loop that never quite lands: Dbmaj7 – C7 – Fm7 (– Ebm7 Ab7), in F minor / A-flat major.
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted, chords only:
- Just the Two of Us — Grover Washington Jr. featuring Bill Withers · 1981: loops Dbmaj7 – C7 – Fm7 – (Ebm7 – Ab7).
- In Japan the same progression is called the "Marusa progression", after Sheena Ringo's song "Marunouchi Sadistic" (1999), and it's all over J-pop.
Why (part 2), all true:
- C7 doesn't belong to the key: it contains E natural, a half step below F. It's a secondary dominant — the "five" of F minor — so it pulls hard into Fm.
- The bass falls a half step, Db → C, then leaps to F: smooth, then decisive.
- Major seventh and minor seventh chords sound soft and jazzy, with no harsh edges.
- Ebm7 – Ab7 is a little ii–V that points back to Dbmaj7, so the loop restarts before it can settle: an endless spiral.
Essence: one borrowed note and a loop that never quite lands — that's why it feels smooth forever.

# 133 — How a Synthesizer Makes Sound (slug: synthesizer)
Idea: oscillator, filter, envelope — the three building blocks of subtractive synthesis.
Songs (part 1) — play only your own demo sounds built with the engine's tone option (sine; saw = all harmonics at 1/n; square = odd harmonics at 1/n):
- Robert Moog introduced his modular synthesizer in 1964.
- Wendy Carlos's album Switched-On Bach (1968) played Bach on a Moog and won three Grammy Awards.
- The Minimoog (1970) made the synthesizer portable (general).
Why (part 2), all true:
- Oscillator: generates a basic waveform. A sine is pure; a sawtooth contains all harmonics (bright, buzzy); a square contains only odd harmonics (hollow).
- Filter: removes part of the harmonics. A low-pass filter cuts the highs — sweeping its cutoff makes the classic "wow" sound.
- Envelope (ADSR): shapes the volume over time — Attack (how fast it starts), Decay, Sustain (level while held), Release (fade after letting go).
- Subtractive synthesis: start with a rich waveform and carve away — like a sculptor.
- The same principles explain real instruments: a piano has a fast attack and long decay; a violin can have a slow attack.
Essence: one waveform, carved by a filter, shaped by an envelope — every synth sound starts there.
Visual idea: harmonic bars per waveform; a filter line cutting bars; an ADSR shape drawn with a.grid bars.

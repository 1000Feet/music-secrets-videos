# 150 — Jupiter: From Planet to Hymn (slug: jupiter-holst)
Idea: the big central tune of Holst's "Jupiter" became a famous hymn.
Songs (part 1) — PUBLIC DOMAIN but do NOT reconstruct Holst's melody; use E-flat major chords and your own broad melody:
- Gustav Holst, The Planets (composed 1914–16): seven movements, one per planet known to astrology — no Earth, and Pluto wasn't discovered until 1930.
- "Jupiter, the Bringer of Jollity": its slow central tune was adapted in 1921 as the hymn "I Vow to Thee, My Country" (tune name: "Thaxted").
- "Mars, the Bringer of War" is in 5/4 (see our 5/4 video).
Why (part 2), all true:
- The outer sections are fast, syncopated and festive — "jollity".
- In the middle, everything slows into a broad, singable melody in E-flat major: a hymn hidden inside a dance.
- A wide melodic range and stepwise motion with a few big leaps make it feel noble.
- Each planet gets its own character through orchestration and rhythm: music as personality.
Essence: a party, then a hymn — and one tune travelled from the stars to the church.
Visual idea: seven planet cards; tempo meter switching fast → slow → fast.

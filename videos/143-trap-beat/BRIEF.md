# 143 — How a Trap Beat Works (slug: trap-beat)
Idea: booming 808 bass, a half-time snare, and rattling hi-hat rolls.
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted: play only your own trap-style beat:
- Trap grew out of Southern hip hop in Atlanta in the 2000s; T.I.'s album "Trap Muzik" (2003) helped name it.
- Producers such as Lex Luger and Metro Boomin shaped the sound (names only).
Why (part 2), all true:
- Tempo around 140 BPM, but the snare (or clap) lands only on beat 3 of each bar — a half-time feel, so it sounds like 70.
- The 808: a deep kick with a long, pitched tail that works as the bass line (see our TR-808 video).
- Hi-hats run in fast 16ths, then switch to rolls in 32nds and triplets — the signature "rattle".
- Sparse dark melodies (often minor) sit on top, leaving space for the bass and the vocals.
- Fast hats over a slow snare: two tempos felt at once.
Essence: a slow, heavy backbeat under racing hi-hats — trap lives in two speeds at once.
Visual idea: circle:false 16-step lanes; hat lane switching to 32nd/triplet rolls; 808 notes as long bars.

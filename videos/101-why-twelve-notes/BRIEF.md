# 101 — Why Are There 12 Notes? (slug: why-twelve-notes)
Idea: stack perfect fifths and you almost come back where you started after exactly 12.
Songs (part 1):
- The piano keyboard: 7 white + 5 black keys, repeating every octave.
- Play Amazing Grace (PUBLIC DOMAIN) on the black keys only, in G-flat: Db Gb Bb Gb Bb Ab Gb Eb Db — the five black keys form a pentatonic scale.
- Most music worldwide in the Western tradition uses these 12 notes (general).
Why (part 2), all true:
- Start on C and go up by perfect fifths (frequency ratio 3:2): C G D A E B F# C# G# D# A# E# B#. After 12 fifths you land on B#, which is almost C, seven octaves higher.
- Almost: it's a little sharp — about a quarter of a half step. That gap is called the Pythagorean comma.
- 12 is the first number of fifths that comes this close to closing the circle. That's why the octave has 12 notes, and why the circle of fifths closes.
- 5 consecutive fifths give the pentatonic scale (the black keys), 7 give the major scale (the white keys). Twelve fill in everything.
- The legend credits Pythagoras (6th century BC) with discovering these ratios.
Essence: twelve notes aren't a convention — they're where the fifths almost come home.

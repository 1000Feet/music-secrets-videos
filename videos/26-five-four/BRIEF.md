# 26 — 5/4 Time (slug: five-four-time)
Idea: five beats per bar instead of four. Usually felt as 3 + 2.
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted except Holst; play only a generic 5/4 groove (no melodies):
- Take Five — The Dave Brubeck Quartet · 1959 (written by Paul Desmond): 5/4, felt as 3 + 2.
- Mission: Impossible theme — Lalo Schifrin · 1966: 5/4.
- Mars, the Bringer of War — Gustav Holst · The Planets · 1914–16: a relentless 5/4 rhythm (public domain; a repeated-note 5/4 ostinato on G is fine).
Why (part 2), all true:
- The brain groups beats into twos and threes; five can't split evenly, so it becomes 3 + 2 (or 2 + 3).
- That uneven grouping feels like a limp or a lean forward — restless, suspenseful, perfect for spies and war.
- Visual idea: a 5-cell grid lighting beat by beat with accents on 1 and 4 (3 + 2).
Essence: one extra beat breaks the symmetry — and tension appears out of pure counting.

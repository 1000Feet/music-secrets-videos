# 154 — Jesu, Joy of Man's Desiring (slug: jesu-joy)
Idea: a flowing triplet melody wrapped around a slow hymn tune — two layers at once.
Songs (part 1) — PUBLIC DOMAIN but do NOT reconstruct either melody; use G major chords, your own flowing triplet line and your own slow hymn-like line:
- From Bach's cantata "Herz und Mund und Tat und Leben", BWV 147 (1723).
- The slow hymn tune (sung by the choir in long notes) is older: "Werde munter, mein Gemüte" by Johann Schop (1642).
- The English title and much of its fame come from pianist Myra Hess's piano arrangement (1926).
Why (part 2), all true:
- Two layers: the orchestra plays a constant, flowing triplet line; the choir sings the hymn in long, slow notes between and over it.
- Setting an existing hymn tune (a chorale) in slow notes inside new music was a common technique in Bach's time.
- The triplets never stop, giving a feeling of calm, endless motion; the hymn gives it a solid, singable core.
- It's counterpoint: two independent ideas that fit together (see our rounds and fugue videos).
Essence: a river of triplets around a simple hymn — motion and stillness at the same time.
Visual idea: two lanes (TRIPLETS / HYMN) with different note speeds; chords on the circle in G major.

# 34 — Moonlight Sonata, decoded (slug: moonlight-sonata)
Public domain — can be played freely.
Facts: Ludwig van Beethoven, Piano Sonata No. 14 in C# minor, Op. 27 No. 2, 1801. The nickname "Moonlight" came from the critic Ludwig Rellstab, years later (Beethoven never used it).
First movement: a constant flow of triplet arpeggios over slow bass notes; opening C#m triplets: G#3 C#4 E4 repeated over bass C#2/C#3; then the harmony moves C#m → C#m/B → A → D/F# (the D major chord is the Neapolitan chord of C# minor) → G#7 → C#m.
Facts usable in part 2:
- The Neapolitan chord (built on the lowered second degree: D major in C# minor) gives that dark, sinking colour early in the piece.
- Beethoven asks for the pedal held so the sounds blur together (sempre pianissimo e senza sordino), creating the misty texture.
- The steady triplets never stop — the melody floats on top almost like a voice.
Essence: one rhythm that never stops, one dark borrowed chord — and a piece that sounds like moonlight, though Beethoven never called it that.

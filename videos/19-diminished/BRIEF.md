# 19 — The Diminished Chord (slug: diminished-chord)
Idea: stack minor thirds: Cdim = C Eb Gb; Cdim7 = C Eb Gb A (Bbb written as A). The classic "villain" / suspense chord.
Examples (part 1):
- Silent-film villain: a trembling diminished seventh chord (generic; public domain idea).
- Toccata and Fugue in D minor — J.S. Bach (attributed) · public domain: the opening flourish famously crashes onto a diminished seventh chord. You may play a dim7 arpeggio and the chord.
- Also usable: horror/suspense scores use it constantly (no specific titles).
Why (part 2), all true:
- Dim7 = four minor thirds, which split the octave into four equal parts: on the circle it is a perfect SQUARE (draw with a.poly / chord shape).
- Because it is symmetric, the same four notes can be read from any of its notes as root — so it can resolve to four different keys (e.g. Cdim7 can lead to Db, E, G or Bb minor/major via its notes acting as leading tones).
- Rotate the square by one step and you get a new chord; by three steps and you are back where you started — there are only THREE different diminished seventh chords.
- It contains two tritones at once (C–Gb and Eb–A): double tension.
Essence: the chameleon chord — perfectly balanced, belonging nowhere, leading anywhere.

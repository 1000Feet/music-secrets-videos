# 98 — The Amen Break (slug: amen-break)
Idea: a seven-second drum solo from 1969 became one of the most sampled pieces of audio ever.
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted: do NOT reproduce the exact break; program your own similar breakbeat (syncopated kicks, snares on 2 and 4 with ghost notes):
- Amen, Brother — The Winstons · 1969, the B-side of their single "Color Him Father". Drummer Gregory Coleman plays a four-bar drum break.
- Sampled in thousands of tracks: hip hop, and especially jungle and drum & bass in the 1990s.
- The band never received royalties for those samples; in 2015 a crowdfunding campaign raised about £24,000 for the band's leader, Richard Spencer.
Why (part 2), all true:
- Funk drumming: kicks off the beat, snare backbeat on 2 and 4, soft "ghost notes" in between — it already swings.
- In the breaks Coleman varies the pattern: the snare lands late, the kick shifts — little surprises inside a loop.
- Producers sped it up (to around 160–180 BPM in jungle), chopped it into single hits and rearranged them — a whole new instrument.
- Short, clean, unaccompanied drums: perfect for sampling.
Essence: seven seconds of drumming, chopped and rearranged — and it built whole genres.
Visual idea: circle:false 16-step grids for 4 bars; then the same hits chopped and reordered faster.

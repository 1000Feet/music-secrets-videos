# 79 — Pictures at an Exhibition: the Promenade (slug: promenade)
Idea: music that walks — with an uneven step: bars of 5 and 6 beats.
Songs (part 1) — PUBLIC DOMAIN, but do NOT reconstruct Mussorgsky's melody from memory; play bar grids alternating 5/4 and 6/4 with your own simple chordal call-and-response in B-flat major (one voice, then full chords):
- Modest Mussorgsky, Pictures at an Exhibition (1874), for piano, inspired by an exhibition of paintings by his friend Viktor Hartmann, who had died in 1873.
- The Promenade represents the visitor walking from one painting to the next; it returns between pictures in different moods.
- Maurice Ravel's orchestration (1922), opening with a solo trumpet, made it world famous.
Why (part 2), all true:
- The opening alternates bars of 5/4 and 6/4: 5 + 6 = 11 beats before the pattern repeats. It feels like an irregular, real walk — someone strolling, looking, pausing.
- Call and response: a single line states the tune, then full chords answer it, like a Russian choir.
- The theme is meant to sound Russian, like a folk song, rather than a European march with regular 4/4 bars.
- Each time it returns, it's changed (shorter, softer, sadder) — the visitor's mood changing after each painting.
Essence: an uneven step and a simple tune — and you're walking through a gallery.

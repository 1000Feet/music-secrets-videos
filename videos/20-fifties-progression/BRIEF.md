# 20 — The 50s Progression (slug: fifties-progression)
Idea: I – vi – IV – V. In C: C, Am, F, G. Also called the doo-wop progression.
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted, play only chords:
- Stand By Me — Ben E. King · 1961 · in A: A, F#m, D, E.
- Every Breath You Take — The Police · 1983: I – vi – IV – V in the verse (do not state the key).
- Earth Angel — The Penguins · 1954: I – vi – IV – V (do not state the key).
Why (part 2), all true:
- C → Am share two notes (C, E). Am → F share two notes (A, C). So the chords glide: only one note moves each time.
- F → G shares no notes, but the bass steps up one note and G (the five chord) pulls straight back to C — the loop closes itself.
- Bass line: C, A, F, G — down a third, down a third, up a step.
- Same four chords as the famous I–V–vi–IV, just in a different order (the order changes the mood: 50s sweetness vs modern anthem).
Essence: chords that share notes feel like one long breath — that's why this loop sounds so warm and safe.

# 138 — Falsetto (slug: falsetto)
Idea: a light, high vocal register above the normal "chest" voice.
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted: no melodies; use your own short phrases jumping between a chest-range line and a high line:
- Stayin' Alive — Bee Gees · 1977: sung in Barry Gibb's falsetto.
- Kiss — Prince · 1986: falsetto lead vocal.
- Sherry — Frankie Valli & the Four Seasons · 1962: famous falsetto hook.
Why (part 2), all true:
- In the normal (modal or chest) register, the vocal folds vibrate along their full thickness.
- In falsetto, only the thin edges of the vocal folds vibrate, so the sound is lighter, breathier and higher.
- That's why men can sing in a range normally associated with women's voices — the voice sounds more fragile and intimate.
- The "break" where the voice flips between registers is part of the effect — yodelling exploits it on purpose.
- In classical music, countertenors use a related technique.
Essence: let the voice go light — and a whole new register opens up.
Visual idea: vocal range bars (chest range vs falsetto range) over the keyboard; a "break" marker.

# 56 — AABA Song Form (slug: aaba-form)
Idea: the 32-bar song form: A (8 bars), A (8 bars), B — the bridge (8 bars), A (8 bars).
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted, play only generic chords:
- Over the Rainbow — Harold Arlen · 1939: AABA.
- Yesterday — The Beatles · 1965: verse, verse, bridge, verse — an AABA shape (the bridge and last verse then repeat).
- Many jazz standards and Tin Pan Alley songs of the 1920s–50s use 32-bar AABA (general).
Why (part 2), all true:
- Repetition (A, A) makes the melody familiar; the bridge (B) brings contrast — often a new key area or different chords — so the return of A feels like coming home.
- The bridge is sometimes called the "middle eight".
- Grid visual: four blocks A A B A, 8 bars each, lighting in sequence.
Essence: say it, say it again, take a detour, come home — the shape of a story in 32 bars.

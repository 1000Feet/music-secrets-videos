# 44 — The William Tell Gallop, decoded (slug: william-tell)
Public domain — can be played freely.
Facts: Gioachino Rossini, William Tell Overture, 1829 — the finale ("March of the Swiss Soldiers"). Later famous as the theme of The Lone Ranger on radio and TV.
Rhythm: the galloping figure short-short-long (da-da-DUM) repeated very fast; melody in E major (simplified, can be played in C if easier): repeated notes on the gallop rhythm rising through the chord.
Facts usable in part 2:
- The rhythm copies a horse's gallop: two quick hoofbeats and a stronger one — rhythm alone tells the story.
- Fast repeated notes + trumpet fanfare + a simple I–V harmony = pure excitement; no complex harmony needed.
- Played at high speed the brain hears it as forward motion — that's why it became the sound of the chase.
Essence: three notes of rhythm, repeated fast enough, can sound like a whole cavalry.

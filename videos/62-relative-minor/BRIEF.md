# 62 — Same Chords, Sad Song: the Relative Minor (slug: relative-minor)
Idea: every major key shares all its notes with a minor key whose home is the sixth note. C major and A minor: same seven white keys, different home.
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted, chords only:
- Let It Be — The Beatles · 1970 · in C: C – G – Am – F (I–V–vi–IV), sounds hopeful.
- Zombie — The Cranberries · 1994: Em – C – G – D. The same family of chords (in G major) but it starts on the minor chord: vi–IV–I–V, so it sounds dark.
- Despacito — Luis Fonsi ft. Daddy Yankee · 2017: Bm – G – D – A, again vi–IV–I–V (D major's chords, starting on B minor).
Why (part 2), all true:
- Rotate C G Am F to start on Am: Am F C G. Same four chords, different first chord — and the mood flips.
- The ear treats the chord you start and keep returning to as home.
- A minor home chord has a minor third (A to C, three half steps) instead of a major third (four half steps) — that one half step is the "sad" colour.
- That's why C major and A minor share a key signature: no sharps, no flats. Every major key has a relative minor three half steps below.
Essence: happy or sad isn't in the notes — it's where you decide home is.

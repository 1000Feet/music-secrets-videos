# 124 — Modal Jazz: So What (slug: so-what)
Idea: fewer chords, more space — improvising on a mode instead of racing through chord changes.
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted: do NOT play the bass theme or the solos; play only generic quartal (stacked-fourth) chords and Dorian scales:
- So What — Miles Davis · from Kind of Blue (1959). Form AABA, 32 bars: 16 bars on D Dorian, 8 bars on E-flat Dorian, 8 bars back on D Dorian.
- The bass states the theme and the band answers with a two-chord figure, played by pianist Bill Evans as stacked fourths — the "So What chord".
Why (part 2), all true:
- In bebop, chords change every bar or two, so soloists race through them. Modal jazz holds one mode for many bars.
- D Dorian = the white keys from D to D. Instead of following chords, soloists explore the colours of one scale.
- The "So What chord": three perfect fourths stacked with a major third on top (e.g. E A D G B). Fourths sound open and ambiguous — neither clearly major nor minor.
- The shift up a half step to E-flat Dorian in the bridge feels like a sudden change of light.
Essence: hold one mode, leave space — and improvisation becomes exploration.
Visual idea: circle showing D Dorian dots; the So What chord as stacked fourths; bridge shifting the whole scale up one step.

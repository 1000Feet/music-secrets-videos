# 100 — 6/8: The Rocking Rhythm (slug: six-eight)
Idea: 6/8 has six quick notes per bar grouped 3+3 — two big beats, each divided in three (ONE-and-a TWO-and-a). 3/4 also has six eighth notes, but grouped 2+2+2.
Songs (part 1):
- Greensleeves — English, 16th century, PUBLIC DOMAIN, usually in 6/8; you may play its first phrase, in A minor: A4 | C5 D5 | E5 F5 E5 | D5 B4 | G4 A4 B4 | C5 A4 | A4 G#4 A4 | B4 G#4 | E4.
- The House of the Rising Sun — traditional; The Animals' 1964 version is in 6/8 (Am C D F Am C E). Do not play the Animals' arrangement; chords only.
- Irish jigs and many lullabies and boat songs (barcarolles) are in 6/8 (general).
Why (part 2), all true:
- Same six eighth notes: in 3/4 they're three beats of two (ONE-and TWO-and THREE-and); in 6/8 two beats of three (ONE-and-a TWO-and-a). Grouping changes everything.
- The two big beats feel like a swing or a rocking boat: down... up... — that's why lullabies and barcarolles use it.
- Each beat divided in three gives a lilting, rolling feel, close to a shuffle.
- Musicians call 6/8 "compound duple": two beats, each compound (made of three).
Essence: the same six notes, grouped in threes — and the music starts to rock.
Visual idea: circle:false grids of 6 cells grouped 2+2+2 vs 3+3.

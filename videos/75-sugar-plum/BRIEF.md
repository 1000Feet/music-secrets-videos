# 75 — The Sugar Plum Fairy (slug: sugar-plum)
Idea: the sound of magic comes from an instrument: the celesta.
Songs (part 1) — PUBLIC DOMAIN, but do NOT reconstruct Tchaikovsky's melody from memory; play the harmony in E minor (Em – B7) in short, plucked, staccato chords and your own short, high, sparkly original motif:
- Dance of the Sugar Plum Fairy, from Tchaikovsky's ballet The Nutcracker (premiered in St Petersburg, December 1892).
- Tchaikovsky discovered the celesta in Paris in 1891 (invented by Auguste Mustel) and asked his publisher to keep it secret, so other Russian composers wouldn't use it before him.
Why (part 2), all true:
- A celesta looks like a small piano, but its hammers strike metal plates instead of strings. The result: a bell-like, glassy tone.
- Timbre is about overtones: the same note on different instruments has a different mix of overtones; metal plates give bright, shimmering ones that fade fast.
- The dance is in a minor key, quiet, with plucked (pizzicato) strings underneath — mysterious rather than sweet.
- High register + short notes + metallic sparkle = "fairy dust".
Essence: the same notes on a different instrument become a different world — timbre is magic.

# 122 — The Walking Bass (slug: walking-bass)
Idea: in jazz, the bass plays one note on every beat, "walking" through the chords.
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted recordings: play only your own walking lines over standard progressions (e.g. ii–V–I in C, 12-bar blues in F):
- Paul Chambers played bass on Miles Davis' Kind of Blue (1959).
- Walking bass is the backbone of swing, bebop and jazz blues (general).
Why (part 2), all true:
- One note per beat (quarter notes) — a steady pulse the whole band can lock onto.
- On strong beats (1 and 3) the bass usually plays chord tones, often the root on beat 1 — so you hear the harmony change.
- On weaker beats it uses passing notes (scale steps between chord tones).
- On beat 4 it often plays an "approach note": a half step above or below the next chord's root, pulling into it.
- The line is both rhythm and harmony: a moving map of the chords.
Essence: one note per beat, always heading somewhere — the bass walks, and the band follows.
Visual idea: walker on the circle following the bass notes; grid of 4 beats per bar with chord tones highlighted.

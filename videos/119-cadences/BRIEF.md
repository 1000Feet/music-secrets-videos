# 119 — Four Ways to End a Phrase: Cadences (slug: cadences)
Idea: musical punctuation: full stop, amen, comma, surprise.
Songs (part 1) — PUBLIC DOMAIN, you may play these:
- Ode to Joy (Beethoven): the first phrase ends on the second note of the scale over the five chord (a half cadence — a comma): E E F G G F E D C C D E E D D(long). The second phrase ends on the home note (an authentic cadence — a full stop): E E F G G F E D C C D E D C C(long). (In C.)
- Hymns traditionally end with "A-men" sung on IV – I: the plagal cadence.
Why (part 2), all true:
- Authentic cadence: V → I. The strongest ending — a full stop.
- Plagal cadence: IV → I. Softer, the "Amen" ending.
- Half cadence: a phrase that stops on V. It sounds open — a comma or a question.
- Deceptive cadence: V → vi. You expect home and get a surprise (we saw it in Hallelujah).
- Composers pair phrases: question (half cadence) + answer (authentic cadence) — exactly like Ode to Joy.
Essence: music speaks in sentences — and cadences are its punctuation.
Visual idea: four cards (FULL STOP, AMEN, COMMA, SURPRISE) with the chord moves on the circle.

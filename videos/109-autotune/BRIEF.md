# 109 — Auto-Tune (slug: autotune)
Idea: pitch correction pulls a sung note to the nearest note of a scale. Turned to the maximum, it creates a robotic, stepping voice.
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted: no melodies; demonstrate with your own sliding line:
- Antares Auto-Tune, released in 1997, was invented by Andy Hildebrand, an engineer who had previously worked analysing seismic data for oil exploration.
- Believe — Cher · 1998: the first big hit to use it as an obvious effect, nicknamed the "Cher effect".
- T-Pain made the hard-tuned voice his signature in the 2000s (general).
Why (part 2), all true:
- A natural voice glides between notes and wobbles (vibrato); its pitch is a smooth curve.
- Auto-Tune measures the pitch and moves it to the nearest note of the chosen scale.
- With a slow setting, it gently nudges notes into tune; at the fastest setting ("retune speed" at zero), the voice jumps instantly from note to note — the slides become steps.
- Visual: a smooth pitch curve over the chromatic circle/keyboard vs the same curve snapped to scale notes (a staircase).
Essence: smooth slides snapped to steps — a mistake-fixer that became a new instrument.

# 96 — Bossa Nova: The Girl from Ipanema (slug: bossa-nova)
Idea: samba's rhythm, softened, plus jazz chords.
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted: chords only, no melody:
- The Girl from Ipanema — Antônio Carlos Jobim & Vinícius de Moraes · 1962 · in F: Fmaj7 | Fmaj7 | G7 | G7 | Gm7 | Gb7 | Fmaj7 | Gb7. The 1964 version by Stan Getz and João Gilberto (with Astrud Gilberto) won the Grammy for Record of the Year.
- Bossa nova ("new trend") was born in Rio de Janeiro in the late 1950s; João Gilberto's guitar style defined it.
Why (part 2), all true:
- João Gilberto's guitar: the thumb plays the bass steadily on the beat, like samba's big drum, while the fingers pluck chords in a syncopated pattern — two layers on one guitar.
- The harmony is jazz: major sevenths (Fmaj7), and G7 — a secondary dominant, the "five of five".
- Gb7 is a tritone substitution: it replaces C7 and slides down by a half step into Fmaj7.
- The bridge jumps up a half step to Gbmaj7 — a surprising, sunny lift.
- Soft dynamics, almost whispered singing: cool rather than loud.
Essence: samba's heartbeat, jazz's colours, and a whisper — that's bossa nova.

# 92 — The Millennial Whoop (slug: millennial-whoop)
Idea: a melody that bounces between the fifth and the third of the major scale (sol and mi), usually sung "wa-oh-wa-oh".
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted songs: do not play specific songs; use your own wa-oh pattern (G–E in C) over I–V–vi–IV:
- In 2016 Patrick Metzger named it the "Millennial Whoop" in an article listing many pop hits of the 2000s and 2010s that use it.
- The same sol–mi interval is the classic playground chant (sol mi la sol mi: "nya nya nya-nya nya") (traditional).
Why (part 2), all true:
- Sol–mi is a falling minor third, and both notes belong to the home chord: no tension, instantly singable.
- Music-education methods like Kodály's start children with exactly this sol–mi interval because it's so easy to sing.
- Sung on an open vowel ("oh"), it works for a whole stadium singing along.
- Both notes also fit many other chords of a typical pop progression, so it can repeat over the whole loop.
Essence: the simplest interval in the world, on the simplest vowel — and a whole crowd can sing it.

# 77 — Dvořák's New World Largo (slug: new-world-largo)
Idea: one of the most nostalgic melodies ever written uses only five notes: the pentatonic scale.
Songs (part 1) — PUBLIC DOMAIN, you may play this melody:
- Antonín Dvořák, Symphony No. 9 "From the New World" (1893), written while he lived in the United States; premiered in December 1893 at Carnegie Hall, New York.
- The second movement, Largo: the English horn solo. Opening in D-flat major (solfège mi sol sol, mi re do, re mi sol mi re): F Ab Ab, F Eb Db, Eb F Ab F Eb. Slow, long notes.
- In 1922 Dvořák's student William Arms Fisher set words to it as the song "Goin' Home" (don't quote the lyrics).
Why (part 2), all true:
- The melody uses only notes of the major pentatonic scale (Db Eb F Ab Bb) — no half steps, so nothing clashes, and it sounds like a folk song.
- Dvořák was deeply interested in African-American spirituals (his student Harry T. Burleigh sang them for him) and in Native American music.
- The English horn — a lower, darker oboe — gives it a warm, melancholy voice.
- Slow tempo (Largo), long held notes, and simple stepwise motion feel like remembering home.
Essence: five notes, no clashes, a dark reed voice — and everyone feels homesick.

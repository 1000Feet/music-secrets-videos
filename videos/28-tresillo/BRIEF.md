# 28 — The Tresillo (slug: tresillo)
Idea: eight pulses grouped 3 + 3 + 2 (accents on pulses 1, 4, 7). From Afro-Cuban and West African traditions.
Examples (part 1):
- Habanera from Carmen — Georges Bizet · 1875 · public domain: the habanera rhythm (built on the tresillo) and the famous slowly descending chromatic melody (D5 C#5 C5 B4 Bb4 A4 … can be played).
- Shape of You — Ed Sheeran · 2017 · copyrighted: its marimba groove is built on the 3 + 3 + 2 pattern (rhythm only, no melody).
- Reggaeton's dembow beat grows from the same cell (general statement).
Why (part 2), all true:
- 3 + 3 + 2 = 8: the pattern pushes against the steady 4 beats, then lands back on beat one — tension and release in rhythm.
- It travelled from Africa through Cuba into the habanera, tango, rock 'n' roll and today's pop.
- Visual idea: 8-cell grid, cells 1, 4, 7 lit; a 4/4 kick underneath for contrast.
Essence: three, three, two — the off-balance heartbeat under half of today's hits.

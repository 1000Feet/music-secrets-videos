# 22 — The Borrowed Minor Four (slug: borrowed-minor-four)
Idea: in a major key, play the IV chord as minor (iv). In G major: C becomes C minor (E → Eb).
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted, play only chords:
- Creep — Radiohead · 1992 · in G: G, B, C, Cm (I – III – IV – iv).
- In My Life — The Beatles · 1965 · in A: the minor iv chord (Dm) appears in the chorus/bridge of an A major song.
Why (part 2), all true:
- Cm is "borrowed" from G's parallel minor key (G minor), where the four chord is naturally minor.
- Only one note changes: E drops a half step to Eb — and Eb then slides down another half step to D, the fifth of G, as the song returns home. A tiny chromatic slide = a lump in the throat.
- In Creep even the B major chord (III) is outside the key (it contains D#), so the whole progression keeps leaving and re-entering the key.
- It sounds bittersweet: major home, minor shadow.
Essence: borrow one note from the sad side, and a happy song suddenly aches.

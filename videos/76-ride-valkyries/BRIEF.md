# 76 — Ride of the Valkyries (slug: ride-valkyries)
Idea: a rhythm that gallops through the sky.
Songs (part 1) — PUBLIC DOMAIN; do not reconstruct the exact theme from memory: play your own B minor arpeggio pattern (B–D–F#) in the galloping long–short–long rhythm, in 9/8, over swirling string figures (fast repeated trills/runs):
- Richard Wagner, Die Walküre (premiered 1870, Munich): the Ride opens Act 3. The Valkyries ride through the air to the mountain rock.
- Famously used in the helicopter attack scene of Apocalypse Now (1979).
Why (part 2), all true:
- The meter is 9/8: three big beats per bar, each divided in three. That lilting 1-2-3 / 1-2-3 / 1-2-3 is the motion of a gallop.
- The theme's dotted rhythm (long–short–long) gives each beat a kick, like hooves.
- The theme climbs through the notes of a single chord — an arpeggio — so it sounds like a heroic fanfare.
- It's played by the brass (horns and trombones), the loudest section of the orchestra, while strings and woodwinds swirl like wind around it.
Essence: a galloping rhythm plus a chord turned into a fanfare — and the orchestra takes flight.

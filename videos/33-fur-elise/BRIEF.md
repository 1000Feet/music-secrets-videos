# 33 — Für Elise, decoded (slug: fur-elise)
Public domain — melody can be played freely.
Facts: Ludwig van Beethoven, Bagatelle in A minor, WoO 59, written around 1810; published in 1867, forty years after his death.
Opening (right hand): E5 D#5 E5 D#5 E5 B4 D5 C5 A4 | (left hand A2 E3 A3) C4 E4 A4 B4 | (left E2 E3 G#3) E4 G#4 B4 C5 | (left A2 E3 A3) E4 E5 D#5 E5 D#5 E5 B4 D5 C5 A4.
Facts usable in part 2:
- It rocks on a half step, E – D#: D# is the leading tone of E (the fifth of A minor), so the melody hovers around the dominant before falling home to A.
- The left hand answers with broken chords: A minor, E major (with G#, the leading tone of A) — tension and release in the bass.
- Form: rondo — the famous theme (A) returns between contrasting sections (A B A C A).
- Mystery: nobody knows who "Elise" was. One theory: the manuscript said "Therese" (Therese Malfatti) and was misread — present it as a theory, not a fact.
Essence: a half step, a broken chord and a returning theme — simple pieces stay famous because your ear always knows where home is.

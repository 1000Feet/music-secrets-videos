# 137 — Vibrato (slug: vibrato)
Idea: a regular wobble in pitch that makes voices and strings sound warm and alive.
Songs (part 1) — play only your own demonstrations (engine tone with pitch bend: straight tone vs vibrato):
- Classical singers, violinists and cellists use vibrato on most sustained notes (general).
- Guitarists create it by bending or shaking a string; the "whammy bar" does it mechanically.
Why (part 2), all true:
- Vibrato is a regular, periodic variation of pitch, usually a few times per second (roughly five to seven in classical singing).
- On a violin, the player rocks the fingertip back and forth on the string; singers produce it with the larynx and breath.
- It makes a tone sound richer and helps a solo voice stand out from an orchestra.
- Baroque-music specialists often use vibrato more sparingly, as an ornament.
- Fun naming mix-up: Fender called its pitch-bending arm a "tremolo" arm, and its amplifier volume effect "vibrato" — technically the other way round. Vibrato = pitch; tremolo = volume.
Essence: a tiny wobble in pitch — and a cold note becomes a living voice.
Visual idea: a straight line vs a wavy pitch line over a keyboard key; a volume line for tremolo.

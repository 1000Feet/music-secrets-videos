# 95 — The Reggaeton Beat: Dembow (slug: dembow)
Idea: the reggaeton groove: a kick on every beat ("four on the floor") plus a snare on the tresillo positions — boom, ch-boom, chick.
In 16 steps per bar: kick on 1, 5, 9, 13; snare on 4, 7, 12, 15 (counting from 1).
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted; play only the generic beat:
- Dem Bow — Shabba Ranks · 1990: the Jamaican dancehall track whose rhythm gave the beat its name.
- Producers in Puerto Rico and Panama built reggaeton on it.
- Gasolina — Daddy Yankee · 2004, and Despacito — Luis Fonsi ft. Daddy Yankee · 2017: worldwide reggaeton hits on this beat.
Why (part 2), all true:
- Two layers: the steady kick (4 even beats) and the snare's 3+3+2 pattern — the tresillo again.
- Even against uneven: the ear hears both at once, so the groove sways instead of marching.
- The pattern loops every bar, so it's hypnotic and danceable at a relaxed tempo (roughly 90–100 BPM).
- It connects to a long Caribbean line: tresillo, habanera, dancehall, reggaeton.
Essence: four even kicks, three uneven snares — and the whole world starts to sway.

# 135 — Why Guitars Are Tuned E A D G B E (slug: guitar-tuning)
Idea: standard guitar tuning is all fourths — except one major third — and violins are tuned in fifths.
Songs (part 1) — play the open strings and simple open chords (E2 A2 D3 G3 B3 E4):
- Standard tuning, low to high: E A D G B E.
- Violin: G D A E (fifths). Viola and cello: C G D A (fifths). Double bass: E A D G (fourths).
Why (part 2), all true:
- Between E–A, A–D, D–G and B–E: perfect fourths (5 half steps). Between G and B: a major third (4 half steps).
- Fourths keep a scale and chord shapes within a hand span across neighbouring strings.
- The one major third makes common open chords easy to finger, and makes the two E strings exactly two octaves apart.
- Violin-family instruments are tuned in fifths: wider gaps, fewer strings needed to cover the range, and fingers can reach a fifth on a small neck.
- The double bass is so large that fifths would mean huge stretches, so it is tuned in fourths like the guitar's low strings.
Essence: fourths, one sneaky third, and fifths for the violin — every tuning is a compromise between hands and harmony.
Visual idea: the six strings as rows with intervals labelled; on the circle, arcs of 5 half steps and the single 4.

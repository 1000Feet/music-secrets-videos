# 69 — The Ostinato (slug: ostinato)
Idea: an ostinato (Italian for "obstinate") is a short pattern repeated again and again while the music changes around it.
Songs (part 1):
- Carol of the Bells — melody "Shchedryk" by Mykola Leontovych (1914), a Ukrainian New Year's song. The MELODY is PUBLIC DOMAIN (you may play it; the 1936 English lyrics are not, don't quote them). Its four-note motif, in G minor, 3/4 time: Bb A Bb G, repeated throughout while other voices change around it.
- Mission: Impossible theme — Lalo Schifrin (copyrighted, name only): its driving bass pattern is an ostinato.
- Ravel's Boléro: the snare-drum rhythm never stops (general reference).
Why (part 2), all true:
- The motif spans only a minor third (G to Bb), so it can sit under many different chords.
- Because it never changes, your ear notices everything else that does: new harmonies, new voices, rising volume.
- Repetition builds tension and a hypnotic momentum — the same principle behind riffs and loops in modern music.
Essence: say it again, and again — and repetition turns into momentum.

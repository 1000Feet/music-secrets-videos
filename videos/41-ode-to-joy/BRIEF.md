# 41 — Ode to Joy, decoded (slug: ode-to-joy)
Public domain — can be played freely.
Facts: Ludwig van Beethoven, final movement of Symphony No. 9, premiered in Vienna in 1824. Beethoven was almost completely deaf by then. Since 1985 the melody is the anthem of the European Union (adopted by the Council of Europe in 1972).
Melody in D major (or C for simplicity): E E F G G F E D C C D E E D D | E E F G G F E D C C D E D C C (in C).
Facts usable in part 2:
- Almost every note moves by step to the next — the first phrase stays within five notes (C to G): anyone can sing it.
- The rhythm is plain, even quarter notes; the only surprise is a famous early, syncopated entry in a later phrase (a note that arrives "too soon").
- Simple + singable = universal: that's why it works as an anthem.
Essence: the most famous melody in the world uses only five neighbouring notes — greatness can be very simple.

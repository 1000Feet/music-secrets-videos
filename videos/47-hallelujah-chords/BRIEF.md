# 47 — Hallelujah: the song that explains itself (slug: hallelujah-chords)
Copyrighted (Leonard Cohen, 1984): do NOT play the melody or quote lyrics beyond the short phrase below; play only chords.
Facts: In "Hallelujah" (Leonard Cohen · 1984; famous cover by Jeff Buckley · 1994) the lyrics describe the chords being played at that moment: "the fourth, the fifth, the minor fall, the major lift". In the original key of C: the fourth = F, the fifth = G, the minor fall = A minor, the major lift = F. (Mention the phrase only as these few words.)
Songs (part 1): Hallelujah — Leonard Cohen · 1984 · in C; Jeff Buckley's version · 1994.
Why (part 2), all true:
- Chords on C: C (home), F (the four), G (the five), Am (the six, minor — the "fall"), F (major again — the "lift").
- The lyric is literally music theory sung out loud.
- Going from G to Am instead of back to C is a deceptive move: the ear expects home and gets a minor chord — the emotional "fall".
- Then F, a major chord, lifts the mood again.
Essence: a song that tells you exactly what it's doing — and still makes you cry.

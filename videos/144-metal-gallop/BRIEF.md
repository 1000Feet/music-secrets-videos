# 144 — The Metal Gallop (slug: metal-gallop)
Idea: a driving long–short–short rhythm on palm-muted low strings.
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted: play only your own gallop riff on a low E power chord:
- Iron Maiden's "The Trooper" (1983) and "Run to the Hills" (1982) are famous for the gallop, driven by bassist Steve Harris.
- Compare William Tell (our earlier video): short–short–long. The metal gallop flips it: long–short–short (an eighth note plus two sixteenths).
Why (part 2), all true:
- The gallop pattern: DA-da-da, DA-da-da — one eighth note and two sixteenths per beat.
- It suggests hooves and forward motion, like a horse running.
- Palm muting (resting the picking hand on the strings near the bridge) makes each note short and percussive.
- Played on low power chords (see our power-chord video), it becomes a wall of rhythm.
- Bass, guitars and drums often play the gallop together, which makes it hit even harder.
Essence: long–short–short, again and again — and the band charges forward.
Visual idea: circle:false grid with 4 beats × (1 long + 2 short) cells; compare with the William Tell pattern.

# 117 — The Secret Order of Sharps and Flats (slug: key-signatures)
Idea: key signatures always add sharps and flats in the same order, following the circle of fifths.
Songs (part 1) — PUBLIC DOMAIN, chords/scales only:
- Für Elise is in A minor: no sharps or flats in the key signature.
- Beethoven's Moonlight Sonata (first movement) is in C-sharp minor: four sharps.
- Debussy's Clair de lune is in D-flat major: five flats.
Why (part 2), all true:
- Order of sharps: F C G D A E B ("Father Charles Goes Down And Ends Battle"). Order of flats is the reverse: B E A D G C F.
- Each key a fifth higher adds one sharp: G major has 1 (F#), D major 2 (F#, C#), A major 3... Each key a fifth lower adds one flat: F major has 1 (Bb), Bb major 2...
- A new key a fifth up keeps six of the seven notes and raises just one — which becomes the new leading tone.
- Shortcut: in sharp keys, the key is a half step above the last sharp; in flat keys (except F), the key is the second-to-last flat.
Essence: the sharps and flats aren't random — they march around the circle of fifths, one at a time.
Visual idea: use a.layout to show the circle of fifths, adding sharps clockwise and flats counterclockwise.

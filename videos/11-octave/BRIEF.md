# 11 — The Octave (slug: octave)
Idea: two notes 12 half steps apart sound like "the same note", higher. Frequency doubles: A = 440 Hz, the A above = 880 Hz (ratio 2:1).
Songs (part 1):
- Somewhere Over the Rainbow — Harold Arlen · 1939 · copyrighted: play ONLY the two-note octave leap (e.g. C4 → C5) on the word "Some-where" idea.
- Take Me Out to the Ball Game — Albert Von Tilzer · 1908 · public domain: opening "Take me out" starts with an octave leap DOWN: C5 C4 A4 G4 E4 G4 D4 (you may play this phrase, in C).
Why (part 2), all true and usable:
- 2:1 → every vibration of the low note lines up with exactly two of the high note (Lissajous 2:1 figure: a.lissajous(2, 1, …)).
- Every harmonic of the upper note is already a harmonic of the lower note, so they blend completely.
- That's why note names repeat every 12 keys on the piano (C2, C3, C4 … same name). On the chromatic circle an octave is a full turn back to the same spot.
- When men and women sing a melody together they are often an octave apart, and we hear it as singing "the same tune".
Essence: the octave is the frame that holds every other note; music's "home address" repeats every octave.
Visual ideas: keyboard sparks on C2..C6; lissajous 2:1 vs 3:2 contrast is optional; walker going a full turn around the circle.

# 94 — The Clave and the Bo Diddley Beat (slug: son-clave)
Idea: a five-note rhythm: the son clave (3-2). In one 16-step cycle (often written as two bars): hits on steps 1, 4, 7, 11, 13 (counting from 1).
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted; play only the clave pattern and generic grooves:
- Cuban son and salsa: the clave pattern organises the whole band. The clave is also the name of the two wooden sticks that play it.
- Bo Diddley — Bo Diddley · 1955: the "Bo Diddley beat" is essentially the son clave.
- Not Fade Away — Buddy Holly & the Crickets · 1957, and Faith — George Michael · 1987: also on the Bo Diddley beat.
Why (part 2), all true:
- The "3" side (three hits: steps 1, 4, 7) is the tresillo — 3+3+2 — that we heard before. The "2" side answers with two hits on steps 11 and 13.
- It can be played 3-2 or 2-3; musicians say the band must be "in clave".
- Five hits, unevenly spaced: tension in the first bar, answer in the second, like a question and an answer.
- Its roots are in sub-Saharan African rhythms carried to Cuba.
Essence: five hits, a question and an answer — and a rhythm that travelled from Africa to Cuba to rock 'n' roll.
Visual idea: circle:false 16-step grid (two halves of 8), highlight the 3 side and the 2 side.

# 68 — Rounds: One Melody, Many Voices (slug: rounds)
Idea: in a round, everyone sings the same melody, entering one after another. It works because the melody is built to fit on top of itself.
Songs (part 1) — all PUBLIC DOMAIN, you may play the melodies:
- Frère Jacques — French round, 18th century. In C: C D E C | C D E C | E F G– | E F G– | G A G F E C | G A G F E C | C G3 C– | C G3 C–.
- Row, Row, Row Your Boat — American round, 19th century. In C: C C C D E | E D E F G | C' C' C' G G G E E E C C C | G F E D C.
- Sumer Is Icumen In — England, 13th century: the oldest known round in English.
Why (part 2), all true:
- Every bar of Frère Jacques outlines (or passes through) the same C major chord, so any two bars stacked together agree.
- Layer the voices two bars apart and harmony appears by itself — thirds, fifths, octaves.
- That's counterpoint: independent lines that sound good together. A canon is the stricter, composed version (Pachelbel's Canon, Bach's canons).
Essence: one melody, staggered in time, turns into harmony.
Visual idea: show 2–4 voices entering as stacked lines/bars; the chord shape on the circle stays C.

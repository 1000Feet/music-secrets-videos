# 152 — Morning Mood: a Sunrise That Isn't in Norway (slug: morning-mood)
Idea: Grieg's famous sunrise music actually depicts a morning in the Moroccan desert.
Songs (part 1) — PUBLIC DOMAIN but do NOT reconstruct Grieg's melody; use E major chords and your own pentatonic phrase passed between two instruments:
- "Morning Mood", from Grieg's music for Ibsen's play Peer Gynt (1875) — the same work as In the Hall of the Mountain King.
- In the play, it accompanies a sunrise in the Moroccan desert (Act 4), not in Norway.
Why (part 2), all true:
- The opening phrase uses the notes of the major pentatonic scale (E F# G# B C#), passed back and forth between flute and oboe — calm, folk-like, no clashes.
- The music gradually grows louder and fuller, building to the full orchestra — the sun rising.
- Key: E major, bright and open.
- Passing a phrase between instruments is like birds calling to each other at dawn.
Essence: five notes, two instruments, one slow crescendo — and the sun comes up.
Visual idea: pentatonic dots on the circle; a rising sun/intensity meter; two instrument labels alternating.

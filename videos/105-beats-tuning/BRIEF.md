# 105 — Why Out-of-Tune Notes Wobble (slug: beats-tuning)
Idea: two notes very close in pitch create a pulsing "wah-wah-wah": beats. The pulse rate equals the difference in frequency.
Songs (part 1):
- Tuning a guitar or violin by ear: musicians listen for the wobble and turn the peg until it slows down and disappears (general).
- Honky-tonk piano: the strings of each note deliberately detuned for a jangly sound (general).
- French musette accordion: reeds tuned slightly apart on purpose for a shimmering tone (general).
Why (part 2), all true:
- 440 Hz and 442 Hz together: the waves drift in and out of step, so the volume pulses twice per second (442 − 440 = 2).
- The closer the notes, the slower the beats; at exactly the same frequency, they vanish.
- Piano tuners listen to and count beats to set every interval.
- Most piano notes have two or three strings; tiny differences between them make the sound richer and warmer — a natural "chorus".
- A little detuning sounds lush; a lot sounds sour.
Essence: the wobble you hear is two waves arguing — tune them, and they agree.
Visual idea: two sine waves slightly apart, the sum's amplitude pulsing; a beat counter.

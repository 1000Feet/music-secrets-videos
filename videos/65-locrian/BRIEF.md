# 65 — Locrian: the Mode Nobody Uses (slug: locrian)
Idea: start the white keys on B: B C D E F G A. Its home chord is B diminished (B D F): a flat second and a flat fifth.
Songs (part 1):
- Autumn Leaves (Joseph Kosma, 1945; copyrighted — chords only): its F#m7b5 (half-diminished) chord. Jazz players use the Locrian mode over half-diminished chords like it, in the minor ii–V–i.
- The B diminished chord inside any C major song: it's the only chord in the key whose fifth isn't perfect.
- Heavy metal guitarists borrow its flat second and flat fifth for menacing riffs (general).
Why (part 2), all true:
- A home chord needs a perfect fifth to feel stable. B to F is a tritone — six half steps — so the "home" chord itself is unstable.
- The flat second (C, a half step above B) pulls away from home instead of toward it.
- So Locrian keeps wanting to resolve somewhere else — usually to C, where its notes really belong.
- Medieval and Renaissance theorists treated it as mostly theoretical.
Essence: a home that can't sit still — proof that the perfect fifth is what makes a home feel like home.

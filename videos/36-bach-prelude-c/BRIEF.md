# 36 — Bach's Prelude in C, decoded (slug: bach-prelude-c)
Public domain — can be played freely.
Facts: J.S. Bach, Prelude in C major, BWV 846, the first piece of The Well-Tempered Clavier, Book I (1722). 35 bars, almost every bar is one chord played as the same broken pattern.
Pattern bar 1 (C): C4 E4 G4 C5 E5 G4 C5 E5 (twice). Bar 2 (Dm7/C): C4 D4 A4 D5 F5 A4 D5 F5. Bar 3 (G7/B): B3 D4 G4 D5 F5 G4 D5 F5. Bar 4 (C): C4 E4 G4 C5 E5 G4 C5 E5.
Facts usable in part 2:
- No melody — the "melody" is the top note of each chord, moving by step: the voices move as little as possible from chord to chord.
- Near the end the bass holds G for several bars (a dominant pedal) building tension, then a long C (tonic pedal) to finish.
- In 1853 Charles Gounod wrote a melody over it: the famous "Ave Maria" (Bach/Gounod).
Essence: one pattern, 35 chords, nothing extra — harmony alone can sing.

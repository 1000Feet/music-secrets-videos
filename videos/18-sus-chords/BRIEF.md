# 18 — Suspended Chords (slug: sus-chords)
Idea: replace the third with the fourth (Csus4 = C F G) or the second (Csus2 = C D G). No third → neither major nor minor.
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted, play only chord moves:
- Pinball Wizard — The Who · 1969: the intro is built on sus4 chords resolving (e.g. Bsus4 → B).
- Free Fallin' — Tom Petty · 1989: D → Dsus4 → D (the sus4 flicker in the riff).
- Use generic sus4→major resolutions (Dsus4 → D, Asus4 → A); do NOT state song keys.
Why (part 2), all true:
- The name comes from the old technique of suspension: a note from the previous chord is held over ("suspended") and then falls to the chord tone.
- Csus4: the F wants to fall a half step to E → C major. That's tension and release in one chord.
- Without a third the chord has no major/minor colour — open, ambiguous, modern (sus2).
Essence: a question held in the air, and an answer one half step away.

# 88 — The James Bond Chord (slug: bond-chord)
Idea: the final chord of the James Bond Theme: E minor with a major seventh and a ninth: E G B D# F# — Em(maj9).
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted: do not play the theme's melody or guitar riff; play generic chords only:
- The James Bond Theme — written by Monty Norman, arranged by John Barry, first heard in Dr. No (1962). It ends on an Em(maj9) chord.
- Underneath, the vamp is a chromatic line inside E minor: the fifth B rises to C, C#, back to C — a minor "line cliché" (Em, Em(#5), Em6, Em(#5)).
Why (part 2), all true:
- A minor chord (E G B) is dark. Add D#: a major seventh, a half step below the root, a leading tone that wants to resolve to E — and never does. Permanent tension.
- Add F# (the ninth) and the chord becomes lush and wide: dark, elegant, suspicious.
- The chromatic line B–C–C#–C creeps by half steps, like someone sneaking around — suspense.
- Film noir and spy music love the minor-major seventh sound for exactly this reason.
Essence: a minor chord with a note that refuses to resolve — danger, style and suspense in one sound.

# 158 — The Radetzky March: the Audience Plays Too (slug: radetzky-march)
Idea: a march where thousands of listeners become the percussion section.
Songs (part 1) — PUBLIC DOMAIN but do NOT reconstruct the melody; use a 2/4 march grid, D major chords and claps:
- Johann Strauss I composed the Radetzky March in 1848, honouring Field Marshal Joseph Radetzky.
- It traditionally closes the Vienna Philharmonic's New Year's Concert, with the audience clapping along while the conductor directs them — louder, softer, or silent.
Why (part 2), all true:
- March in 2/4: a strong ONE-two, ONE-two pulse that's easy to clap.
- Contrast between loud and soft sections gives the conductor a game to play with the audience.
- A middle section (the trio) changes key and mood, then the main march returns (see our Stars and Stripes video).
- Clapping on the beat turns listeners into performers — the oldest form of participation.
Essence: a steady beat, a few dynamics — and the whole hall joins the orchestra.
Visual idea: circle:false 2/4 grid with clap markers sized by loud/soft; trio block.

# 15 — The Half Step (slug: half-step)
Idea: the smallest interval in Western music, one key to the very next (E to F). Ratio about 16:15.
Songs (part 1):
- Jaws — John Williams · 1975 · copyrighted: ONLY two notes alternating, E2 and F2 (getting faster).
- Für Elise — Ludwig van Beethoven · 1810 · public domain: E5 D#5 E5 D#5 E5 B4 D5 C5 A4 (melody can be played).
- Also true and usable: the leading tone B rises a half step to C (that pull ends almost every song).
Why (part 2), all true:
- Two notes this close make their waves "rub": the interference makes a rough, buzzing beat — your ear hears it as tension.
- Twelve half steps make the octave: the chromatic scale.
- A half step resolving (B → C, F → E) is the strongest "pull" in harmony; a half step that does NOT resolve (Jaws) keeps you waiting… suspense.
- Für Elise rocks back and forth on a half step (E–D#) — gentle tension instead of fear: tempo and loudness change the meaning.
Essence: the smallest step carries the biggest tension — fear, longing, or the final pull home.

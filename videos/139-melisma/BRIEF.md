# 139 — Melisma: Many Notes, One Syllable (slug: melisma)
Idea: singing a run of several notes on a single syllable.
Songs (part 1):
- Handel's Messiah (1742), "For unto us a Child is born": long runs on the word "born" (PUBLIC DOMAIN — do not reconstruct the runs; illustrate with your own run on one syllable).
- Gregorian chant: long melismas on the final "-a" of "Alleluia" (general).
- In gospel and R&B, singers such as Whitney Houston and Mariah Carey made vocal runs a signature (general; copyrighted — no melodies).
Why (part 2), all true:
- Syllabic singing: one note per syllable — clear for telling a story.
- Melismatic singing: one syllable stretched across many notes — the voice takes over from the words.
- Runs often move quickly through a scale (often pentatonic or blues scale in R&B), decorating a simple target note.
- It shows off control and emotion: the word is held while the melody blossoms.
- The same idea appears in many traditions: chant, Baroque arias, Arabic and Indian singing, gospel.
Essence: one syllable, many notes — the moment the voice speaks louder than the words.
Visual idea: one big syllable label while a fast walker runs through scale notes on the circle.

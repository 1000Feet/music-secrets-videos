# 45 — Air on the G String, decoded (slug: air-on-g-string)
Public domain — can be played freely.
Facts: J.S. Bach, "Air" from Orchestral Suite No. 3 in D major, BWV 1068 (around 1730). The nickname comes from August Wilhelmj's 1871 arrangement for violin, which could be played entirely on the violin's lowest string, the G string.
Bass line (walking in octaves, in D): D3 D2 C#3 C#2 B2 B1 A2 A1 G2 G1 F#2 F#1 E2 E1 D2 … (steady eighth notes descending by step).
Melody start: F#5 held long, then F#5 B5 G5 E5 D5 C#5 D5 (very slow, can be approximated).
Facts usable in part 2:
- The bass walks down the scale step by step in octave jumps — a steady heartbeat under a long, floating melody.
- The melody holds very long notes that clash gently with the moving harmony underneath, then resolve: suspensions, one after another.
- It inspired later music built on a walking-down bass, like A Whiter Shade of Pale (1967).
Essence: a bass that never stops walking and a melody that refuses to hurry — calm made of motion.

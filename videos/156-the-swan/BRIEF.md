# 156 — The Swan (slug: the-swan)
Idea: a gliding cello melody over rippling piano — the only piece Saint-Saëns allowed published from his musical joke.
Songs (part 1) — PUBLIC DOMAIN but do NOT reconstruct the cello melody; use G major chords with your own rippling arpeggios and a slow original line:
- Saint-Saëns, The Carnival of the Animals (1886): 14 movements depicting animals.
- He considered it a light-hearted joke and banned its publication during his lifetime — except The Swan, published in 1887. The full work was published after his death (1921).
- The choreographer Michel Fokine created the solo dance "The Dying Swan" for ballerina Anna Pavlova (1905) to this music.
Why (part 2), all true:
- The cello sings a long, smooth melody (legato) — the swan gliding calmly.
- Underneath, the piano (originally two pianos) plays continuous rippling broken chords — the water.
- Key: G major. Slow tempo, long notes, few leaps: grace and calm.
- Two textures at once: a still surface and constant movement beneath — like a swan paddling.
Essence: calm above, ripples below — that's how music draws a swan.
Visual idea: two lanes (CELLO long notes / PIANO ripples); G major on the circle.

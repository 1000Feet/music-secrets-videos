# 61 — The Major Scale (slug: major-scale)
Idea: the major scale is a pattern of whole and half steps: W W H W W W H. In C it is all the white keys: C D E F G A B C.
Songs (part 1):
- Joy to the World — words by Isaac Watts (1719), tune arranged by Lowell Mason (1839). PUBLIC DOMAIN: its opening line IS the major scale going down, all eight notes: C' B A G F E D C (rhythm: long, short-dotted, short, long, then four even notes and a long final C). You may play it.
- Do-Re-Mi — Rodgers & Hammerstein, The Sound of Music (1959): a whole song that teaches the scale's syllables. Copyrighted: do NOT play its melody; just name it.
Why (part 2), all true:
- Only two half steps, between mi–fa (E–F) and ti–do (B–C). That asymmetry tells your ear where home is.
- Ti sits a half step under do, so it pulls up into it: the "leading tone".
- The three main chords C (C E G), F (F A C) and G (G B D) together contain exactly the seven notes of the scale.
- The seven notes are also seven fifths stacked: F C G D A E B.
- The syllables come from Guido d'Arezzo (11th century), who took them from the first syllables of lines of a Latin hymn, "Ut queant laxis" (ut later became do).
Essence: seven notes, two half steps — and that small asymmetry gives every note a role.

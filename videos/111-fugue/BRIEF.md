# 111 — What Is a Fugue? (slug: fugue)
Idea: a fugue builds a whole piece from one short theme (the subject) that voices take turns to play, overlapping.
Songs (part 1) — PUBLIC DOMAIN but do NOT reconstruct Bach's subjects from memory; demonstrate with your own short original subject:
- J. S. Bach, The Well-Tempered Clavier (two books of 24 preludes and fugues each, 48 in total).
- Bach's The Art of Fugue, left unfinished at his death in 1750.
- Bach's The Musical Offering (1747), built on a theme given to him by King Frederick the Great when Bach visited Potsdam.
Why (part 2), all true:
- One voice plays the subject alone. A second voice enters with the "answer": the same subject a fifth higher (or a fourth lower).
- While the answer plays, the first voice continues with a countermelody (the countersubject).
- More voices enter one by one; between entries come "episodes" that move through other keys.
- Near the end, entries often overlap more tightly (stretto), building intensity.
- Entries on the home key and the fifth establish the key; imitation gives the whole piece unity.
Essence: one small idea, layered against itself — and it grows into a cathedral of sound.
Visual idea: stacked voice lanes (circle:false grids) showing entries; on the circle, subject on C then answer on G.

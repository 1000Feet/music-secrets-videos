# 70 — Theme and Variations: Paganini's Caprice No. 24 (slug: theme-variations)
Idea: one short theme, transformed again and again.
Songs (part 1):
- Niccolò Paganini, Caprice No. 24 in A minor, from the 24 Caprices for solo violin, Op. 1 (published 1820) — PUBLIC DOMAIN: a theme, 11 variations and a finale. Do NOT reconstruct the violin melody from memory; play its harmonic skeleton: the theme's first part alternates A minor and E (i – V).
- Brahms, Variations on a Theme of Paganini (1863); Liszt also arranged it.
- Rachmaninoff, Rhapsody on a Theme of Paganini (1934) — copyrighted, name only: in Variation 18 he turns the theme upside down (inversion), slows it down and puts it in D flat major.
Why (part 2), all true:
- The theme is short and built on the simplest harmony: home (A minor) and its five chord (E). A strong skeleton can carry endless flesh.
- A variation can change rhythm, register, speed, mode (minor to major) or turn the melody upside down — and you still recognise the bones.
- Inversion: every interval that went up now goes down, so a nervous minor tune becomes a lush major melody.
Essence: a great theme is a skeleton — the variations are everything you can build on it.

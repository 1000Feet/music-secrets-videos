# 145 — The Chords of Hotel California (slug: hotel-california)
Idea: a looping progression in B minor that uses the harmonic minor's major five chord.
Songs (part 1) — copyrighted: chords only, no melody, no guitar parts:
- Hotel California — Eagles · 1976. Progression: Bm – F#7 – A – E – G – D – Em – F#7.
- It won the Grammy for Record of the Year in 1978. It ends with a long dual-guitar solo by Don Felder and Joe Walsh.
Why (part 2), all true:
- F#7 is the five chord of B minor. It contains A#, the raised seventh — a leading tone pulling into B (see our harmonic minor video).
- A – E and G – D: each pair moves by a fifth, like small steps around the circle of fifths.
- The progression starts dark (Bm), drifts through brighter major chords, then returns via F#7 — tension before every restart.
- An eight-chord loop that never quite feels finished keeps the song hypnotic.
Essence: one raised note and a loop that keeps circling back — a hotel you can never leave.
Visual idea: the 8 chords on the circle with the A# highlighted on F#7; walker on the roots.
